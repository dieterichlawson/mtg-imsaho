## Drafting

**D43, sideboarding between games.** The server's checks all hold:
- It refused a 39-card deck, a card from the other seat's pool, nonbasic and snow lands as basics, sideboarding before game 1 or after ready, and a play/draw choice from the non-chooser.
- A card moved out and back in is not announced as sideboarding.
- When the clock runs out, the decks stand as sent.
- Each game starts from the previous game's deck, and each new match from the registered deck.
- All 62 of the runner's `SIDEBOARD before game` lines match the deck the next game was given.

The problems are what a person meets:
- The play/draw buttons are blank white boxes (#873).
- The AI seats' deck-build prompt still says nothing is sideboarded (#876).
- A person's sideboarded deck and their play/draw choice are recorded nowhere. The hosted game logs have no decklist, so the idea's own check ("read the game log's decklist") cannot be done (#878).

**D44, who plays first.** The engine was right in all 67 games: the coin flip's winner chooses game 1 and the loser chooses after that. The announcement is wrong: "X chose to play first" is printed when the clock chose, or when an AI won the flip and was never asked (#877). No game drew, so the after-a-draw rule was only read in the code. D53 proposes a way to reach it.

**D45, tiebreakers by hand.** On the 8-, 7- and 5-seat tables, byes included, every OMW%, GW% and OGW% matched a hand computation under MTR Appendix C. That holds for FINAL STANDINGS, `/api/view`, the page and the client. The one gap: before round 1 counts, the view still carries `gw: 0.33` (#883).

**D46, the hosted table's snapshot.** Plain Ctrl-C and `--resume` work at every stage tried:
- mid-draft, mid-build, before game 1 and mid-game;
- mid-round 2 with one match done;
- after the tournament.

Keys, pools, picks, names, AI notes and a ready AI deck all survive. A match in progress replays from game 1 with clean standings.

The restore checks only picks (through `Table::pick`) and decks (through `validate_deck`) and trusts everything else. Its "another table" guard compares the snapshot with a lobby built from the same snapshot, so it can never fire. The runner's equivalent holes were closed by #730, #731 and #655, and none of those fixes reached the hosted copy. Thirty-two doctored snapshots found:
- match records taken on faith (#882);
- picks replayed by index onto whatever the seed now deals (#884);
- decks for seats still drafting, second decks and foreign sideboards accepted (#886);
- a panic on an out-of-range seat (#887);
- unchecked `best_of`, seat counts, keys and names (#888);
- a lying "carries on" written into the live table's log (#893).

Two findings need no editing at all:
- A sent-but-not-ready deck is not saved (#890).
- An unwritable `--save` is never reported (#891).

**D47, presence.** The presence words from #822-#825 agree on all five surfaces at every step, and a closed page or a kick is named on both game pages. After a close, every page and client says why and stops reconnecting. The one gap is that views keep going out while the sockets drop, so the two draft pages freeze on different words for the same seat (#895).

**D40, what a round leaves behind.** Partial run. With the game ports exactly one round wide, every match freed its threads and its port, and a finished link says the match is over. Host actions while the opponent is being asked were not tried.

**D50, the host's cli seat.** The cli seat and `mtg-draft-client` print the same screens phase by phase, because both run `client.rs`. Commands go where the manual says, and `--resume` reseats the host. Found:
- A SIGSTOP/SIGCONT (Ctrl-Z then `fg`, or an strace attach) is EINTR, which both loops treat as fatal (#880).
- The pool prints under the pack, so by mid-draft the pack has scrolled off an 80×24 screen (#894).
- Under `--quiet`, a `kick` or `start` that works gets no answer (#905).
- `help` shows the client's usage, including a `quit` that stops the whole server (#906).
- `ready` while drafting is refused twice (#907).
- `kick 0` on your own seat is taken with no confirmation (#909, which may be deliberate).

**D51, the pick review.** The data is exact over 6 snapshots × 2 seats × 42 picks, before and after resumes. Another seat's review is refused. The client's "over …" list drops the second copy of a duplicated card (#904).

**D49, one port.** strace shows the proxy never connects anywhere but a live game port, and traversal reaches nothing. The hole is upstream of the proxy: the game link has no key (#872).

**D52, who the AI seats are.** cc seats are what they claim: a bare `ai` is `claude -p --model claude-haiku-5-5`, and no key reaches any pick or build child. A metered seat is accepted silently (#902). With no key, it panics after the draft has started (#903).

**D48, the nightly loop.** A stub night's JSON agrees with its log, a kill-and-resume plays each match once, and a recorded date is refused with nothing touched. Found:
- The snapshot directory ignores `DRAFT_LOOP_OUT`, so a dry run poisons the real night of that date (#899).
- Card statistics ignore sideboarding (#900).
- "What went wrong" undercounts pick re-asks and draft unreadables (#901).
- An interrupted run prints no usage (#912).

The two real nights' lessons and guide are grounded in their decks.

## The GUI (the draft page)

**G35, the rehearsal at unrun shapes.** 3 humans in 5 seats, best-of-1 and best-of-5 all play clean, including tiebreakers checked by hand and autoplay on its own. An all-human table cannot be asked for: the script sends `0xai`, prints FAIL after 60 s and exits 0 (#871). Neither driver takes a port, so every crew has to patch a copy.

**G32, between games.** The panel's logic holds:
- the score and who chooses;
- a 39-card deck held back, with the reason;
- a reload restores the server's last deck.

What it shows does not:
- The buttons are invisible (#873).
- The game page never draws its "go to your draft page" line, so a person sat on "Waiting for the game…" for the whole 300 s clock (#879).
- At 390 wide, Ready sits under the pool (#885).
- "Add 1 more cards" (#874).

**G34, the review on the page.** It is sound on substance: table picks are marked in words, DFCs read by their front face, and the review pushes nothing off the screen. Every pick is numbered twice, and only by hover can any card be read (#889).

**G33, the table's words.** At 390 wide, names break mid-word, "[1 bye]" splits and the tiebreakers wrap on a dangling "/" (#881). After a reload the name box is empty while the table still uses the name (#892).

## The harness

**H35 and H38, text answers.** A stand-in that answers only from the format line plays 8 coverage games with 0 re-asks, and real Haiku produced 0 unreadables in 69 calls. The reader is brittle, though:
- One stray brace makes a complete answer unreadable (#896).
- A seat that never answers readably plays on fallbacks for ever (#898).
- The PROMPT log omits the format line (#910).
- 0ab9837 drops a required `maxItems: 0` field from the format line, so X funding with nothing untapped is re-asked every time (#897).

**H26, standing check.** The bound holds: 0 resumes, one system prompt per game, and prompt size follows the board.

**H36 and H37.** TTLs are as designed, with steady cache reads in the real games. Any TTL value other than `1h` silently means 5 minutes (#911). The AI seat's sideboard prompt shows only public cards under one schema, but lists a transformed card by its back face alone (#908).
