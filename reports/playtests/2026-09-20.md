## L49 — the characteristic-defining P/T as a value other cards read

Corpse Lunge is the one card in the set that consumes an off-battlefield
power, and both cards it most naturally exiles — Boneyard Wurm and
Splinterfright — are `*/*` equal to the number of creature cards in your
graveyard. The exile is an additional cost paid at announcement (CR 601.2f/h);
the damage is computed on resolution (CR 608.2). So the number can move
between the two, and the card's own exile moves it.

**Decks** (one-off, written outside the repo):

```
l49-lunge   8 Corpse Lunge / 4 Splinterfright / 4 Boneyard Wurm /
            4 Armored Skaab / 4 Forbidden Alchemy / 4 Geist-Honored Monk /
            2 Mulch / 8 Forest / 8 Swamp / 8 Island / 6 Plains
l49-min     12 Boneyard Wurm / 12 Corpse Lunge / 18 Forest / 18 Swamp
l49-monk    12 Geist-Honored Monk / 8 Corpse Lunge / 4 Armored Skaab /
            12 Plains / 12 Swamp / 8 Island / 4 Forest
l49-tree    16 Tree of Redemption / 24 Forest   (0/13 defender — a damage
                                                 sponge you can read exactly)
```

3 hotseat games (seeds 7, 11, 23) to turns 16/13/17, plus 50
`random`-vs-`random` runs (20 seeds `l49-lunge` vs `l49-tree`, 30 seeds
`l49-monk` vs `l49-min`).

### The engine is right, in both directions

**Down.** Graveyard holding exactly one creature card, a Boneyard Wurm, which
therefore reads `1/1` in the picker because it counts itself. Exile it to
Corpse Lunge and the graveyard has no creature cards, so the exiled Wurm is a
0/0 and the spell deals **0**. The Tree took no damage and had no `(Nd)`
marker. That is the CR answer (604.3 — the CDA functions in exile; 108.4 — an
exiled card has no controller, so "your graveyard" is the owner's), and
`corpse_lunge.rs` carries a comment saying the engine used to get exactly this
one wrong.

**Up.** Same shape, changed in response. Corpse Lunge exiling a Splinterfright
(the only creature card, so 0 after its own exile), then Forbidden Alchemy
cast *in response*, milling a Boneyard Wurm into the graveyard. Alchemy
resolves first; Corpse Lunge then resolves and deals **1**:

```
Exiled Splinterfright (#9) from graveyard as additional cost
p0 cast Corpse Lunge (#5) targeting Tree of Redemption (#62)
p0 cast Forbidden Alchemy (#23)
Forbidden Alchemy (#23) resolved
Corpse Lunge (#5) resolved
Corpse Lunge (#5) dealt 1 damage to Tree of Redemption (#62)
```

An announcement-time snapshot would have dealt 0. The resolution-time read is
the right one.

**The other CDA.** Geist-Honored Monk (`*/*` equal to the number of creatures
you control) in the graveyard tracked p0's board live — `4/4` with a Monk, an
Armored Skaab and two Spirit tokens out — and exiling it dealt **4**. So the
consumer is right on a CDA whose input is the battlefield as well as one whose
input is the graveyard.

**Every zone renders it.** The same card read `0/0` in hand with an empty
graveyard, `1/1` in hand and in the graveyard with one creature card there,
`1/1` in the exile pane, `4/4` on the battlefield — and the exile pane got
Splinterfright *and* Boneyard Wurm right simultaneously, which are different
sums. L11's display claim holds at runtime on a fourth zone.

**Incidental, and correct**: Splinterfright cast into an empty graveyard
enters as a 0/0 and dies to CR 704.5a on the spot — `resolved` then `died` in
consecutive log lines — which is the cheapest way to put a creature card in
your own graveyard in this pool.

### What broke: the amount is only narrated when it is nonzero (#553)

`queue_damage` (`damage.rs:137`) and `perform` (`damage.rs:731`) both return
early on `amount == 0`, and both early returns sit **above** the
`"{source} dealt {amount} damage to {target}"` log line. CR 120.8 makes "deals
0 damage" into "does not deal damage at all", so the *rules* outcome is right;
the *account* of it is nothing. The log says `Corpse Lunge (#16) resolved` and
stops.

This is not a corner case for this card — it is the majority outcome. Over 30
`random`-vs-`random` games (`l49-monk` vs `l49-min`, seeds 101–130):

```
cast = 109   fizzled = 19   resolved = 90   dealt = 37   silent = 53
```

**53 of 90 resolutions (59%) leave no record of what the spell did.** A fizzle
*is* logged distinctly (`Corpse Lunge (#74) fizzled (all targets illegal)`),
so the ambiguity is narrower than it first looked — but the player who spent
`{2}{B}` and permanently exiled a creature card is still told only that the
spell resolved. Same shape as #299, which was accepted and fixed for counters.

`queue_damage` is the shared entry point for every simultaneous-damage event,
so this reaches every source whose amount is computed and can come out 0.

### The two negative cases the idea asked for

**A token in the exile picker: unreachable, but the filters disagree about
it.** `sba.rs:359` sweeps `is_token && zone != Battlefield` under CR 704.5d, so
no token can be sitting in a graveyard at the moment a picker is built.
Underneath that, though, `exile_prompt`'s `gy()` filters `is_creature` and
**not** `is_card`, while `additional_cost_is_payable`'s named-ids path *does*
filter `is_card` (`in_own_graveyard_card`). Harmless today, and self-protecting
if it ever were not — a token offered by the picker would be refused as
unpayable rather than exiled — but the two lists do not agree on what a
"creature card in your graveyard" is, and nothing asserts that they should.
Not filed: no observable behaviour.

**The auto-picker ranks by the wrong power, and nothing reaches it.** When a
cast arrives with no exile ids named, `pay_exile_creatures` picks the
highest-`effective_power` candidates, with a comment saying this is for Corpse
Lunge's sake. It ranks by the power *before* the exile, which for a CDA card is
not the power the damage will use: with a Boneyard Wurm and a vanilla 2/2 both
reading 2, exiling the Wurm deals 1 and exiling the 2/2 deals 2, and the
tiebreak is the object id. But the CLI always asks, and the random seat rolls
its own answer (pinned by the tests for #455 and #262), so no seat reaches the
path. Not filed: unreachable from every shipped seat.

**One cosmetic note**, seen verbatim in all three games and not filed on its
own: the exile prompt's two sentences are joined without punctuation —
`choose exactly 1 creature to exile from your graveyard Mark 1 of the 1 cards
below to exile.` — because `rule_title` splits the description at 60 columns
and `cli.rs:6063` joins the remainder to `set_question` with a bare space.

## L47 — the permanent that is a copy, as a thing the player can see

One real Merciless Predator, one Evil Twin clone of it, and three Cackling
Counterpart token copies on the board at once (`10 Island / 10 Swamp /
6 Mountain / 4 Reckless Waif / 5 Evil Twin / 5 Cackling Counterpart` vs
`40 Plains`, seed 4747, hotseat to turn 19), then the same question asked of
each of the six readers; plus 10 `random` mirror games (seeds 1–10) and one
`cli`-vs-`random` control (`14 Plains / 10 Champion of the Parish / 16 Doomed
Traveler`, seed 47).

**The engine held under load.** 41 Evil Twin casts produced 23
enters-as-a-copy and 18 declines, so the random seat rolls that choice; 157
token copies were made; and of 153 werewolf transform triggers pushed, 44
flipped and 109 resolved inert — 86 token (CR 111.7), 12 single-faced clone
(CR 701.28c), 11 off-battlefield (CR 400.7) — **every one logged with its CR
citation**, so #500's fix is holding. Both halves L47 wanted cleared are
clear: `ChooseTriggerOrder` enumerates one `ChosenIndex` per remaining trigger
rather than N! permutations, and the random seat answers it one at a time, 72
times.

**Five of six readers cannot tell the three apart.** The battlefield pane
marks the token `[tok]`, marks the clone nothing, and then *collapses the
clone and the card it copied* into `2x Merciless Predator 3/2` (#557). The `i`
list carries neither `[tok]` nor an id, so three permanents with three
different ability sets are three byte-identical numbered rows (#556). The
stack pane prints no object ids at all — five identical
`Merciless Predator's upkeep trigger` rows, one keystroke after the ordering
screen distinguished all five by id (#555). The `i` detail page is the only
reader that works. The target choosers and the ordering screen do carry ids —
except that the ordering screen's `Sources:` block, the one thing that would
explain why four of those five triggers can do nothing, is silently empty for
**every transformed DFC in the set**, because `ordering_sources` looks the card
up by back-face name and `CardRegistry` indexes only front faces (#558,
A/B'd against Champion of the Parish, which prints its block correctly).

**And one rules bug underneath.** A Cackling Counterpart token copy of an Evil
Twin clone comes out *without* the "except it has `{U}{B}, {T}: Destroy target
creature with the same name`" ability: `create_token_copy` never carries
`copy_grantor`, and that field is the ability's only home (#554, CR 707.2 —
copiable values as modified by other copy effects).

## L48 — the attachment SBA that only knows one of its cases

The idea's premise is **confirmed**, and it remains **dormant**.

All three "arrivals" that would make it live were re-swept against
`mtg-engine/src/cards/` tonight rather than taken on faith from 09-15, and
**none has landed**: `git log --since=2026-09-15 --diff-filter=A` adds no card
file at all; the only protection grants are `ProtectionFromSubtype`
Vampire/Werewolf/Zombie plus Spare from Evil's
`And(HasCardType(Creature), Not(HasSubtype(Human)))`, which no Aura or
Equipment matches; no effect removes a card type; and all 19 Auras are
`TargetRequirement::Creature` or `PlayerOnly`. One datum 09-15 did not have:
`CreatureFilter` has no colour variant at all, so "protection from white" is
not currently expressible, while `HasCardType(Enchantment|Artifact)` **is** —
that is the cheapest way a future card makes this live.

Read side by side, `sba.rs`'s four-line attachment sweep implements **2 of 9**
CR 704.5m/n/702.16c cases and `invariants/permanents.rs::check_settled`
implements **9 of 9**. The engine and its own fuzzing oracle disagree,
dormantly. The filter also opens with `o.attached_to.is_some()` directly under
a comment reading "Rule 704.5m: Aura not attached to anything goes to
graveyard" — the comment names the one case the code cannot reach — and the
function's doc comment promises a panic on that state which the guard makes
unreachable.

The night's deliverable was the test the idea asked for: six cases, an Aura
and an Equipment each on a non-creature host, on a host with protection from
it, and attached to nothing, with each assertion printing `check_settled`'s
contradictory verdict on the identical state. **5 failed, 1 passed, exit 101**
(#552). The file is **not committed** — it would fail
`test_suite_guards::the_testing_guide_names_every_rule_test_file` until
`docs/TESTING.md` names it, and a crew that files does not land engine
changes — so it is inline in #552 in full, with the one-line `docs/TESTING.md`
row whoever commits it will need.
