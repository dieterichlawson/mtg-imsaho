#564 (V46).

The night's shape: **all three probes found the thing they were sent at
behaving correctly, and all three found the failure one step to the side of
it.** V44 was sent to ask whether the progress watchdog can still kill a
healthy game and found it cannot, by a factor of 33 — then found that the
*other* runner cannot stop a stalled one. V45 was sent to measure latency on a
huge board and confirmed the numbers, then found the cost is not where the
idea said it was and is paid by every seat, not the CLI. V46 was sent to
decide whether a lenient CLI reader should be lenient, found all nine of them
uniform and honest, then found the browser page deciding the same question
differently and committing a cast on an empty box.

## The three that matter

**#565 — one keypress costs 16-23 seconds at 1,000 permanents.** The most
consequential find of the night, and the only one with a number a user feels.
A token-flood hotseat reached 12,928 permanents on turn 22; that one position,
pruned to a ladder and resumed rung by rung, puts the per-decision cost at a
clean `n^2.3-2.8` with **no cliff anywhere** — 0.09 s per keypress at 100
permanents, 1.9-3.7 s at 500, 16-23 s at 1,000, 8m30s to reach the first
prompt at 4,000, and at 12,928 no prompt at all: killed after 6m35s of 100%
CPU with a completely blank pane. `utime+stime` from `/proc` tracks wall time
to within 0.1 s, so none of it is I/O.

The idea's own diagnosis was half wrong, and disproving it is what makes this
filable against the engine rather than the TUI. `capture-pane` is instant, so
it is not the renderer — but it is not "the engine advancing the game"
either: `--resume --p1 random --p2 random` of the same 1,000-permanent save
writes its first `--log` line **58 ms** after launch, and then that same run
sits 40+ seconds at `DeclareAttackers` with nobody at a keyboard. The cost is
the per-decision `GameView` and legal-action construction, and **every seat
pays it** — which is why V50 now schedules the same measurement on the fuzzer,
the page and the LLM seat, and asks whether the nightly fuzz job is already
paying it without anyone noticing.

**#559 — the draft runner cannot stop a stalled game at a prompt.** V44's
find, and the one that came from asking a question the idea did not.
`progress_fingerprint` is a contract about *when* the watchdog fires, and it
holds; nobody had ever probed the second contract, which is what the runner
does next. `mtg-runner` calls `die(...)` unconditionally. `mtg-draft-runner`,
the copy that got the watchdog second (#488), does this:

```rust
if stalled || action_count >= max_actions {
    if let Some(concede_idx) = legal.actions.iter()
        .position(|a| matches!(a, Action::Concede)) { return ...; }
}
// falls through and asks the seat again
```

`legal_actions` pushes `Concede` at `engine.rs:302`; `legal_actions_while_awaiting`
returns at `engine.rs:224`, eleven lines earlier. So at **any** prompt the
lookup always misses — and for a set prompt `legal.actions` is empty outright.
The forfeit silently does not happen, the same guard covers the 50,000-action
ceiling, and that loop therefore has no unconditional termination at all. The
engine accepts `Concede` submitted there anyway and ends the game; the runner
simply never sends it. This is the #404/#488 shape a third time — one
protocol, two copies, a guard that reached one of them — which is now V47.

**#561 — a bare Enter on the page commits an X=0 cast.** V46's find, and the
clearest case yet of the "one decision, four surfaces" rule earning its place.
The CLI's nine numeric readers are uniform: all accept `+N` and leading zeros,
all reject `-0`, and all select exactly the value they name — V2's verdict,
re-confirmed against `--log` on eight accepted tokens. Then the page reads the
same prompt's X with JavaScript `Number()`, which takes `-0`, `0x2`, `0b11`,
`2.0` and `"  3  "` where the terminal refuses every one. `Number("")` is 0
and `Number.isInteger(0)` is true, so pressing Enter at the auto-focused,
initially-empty box announces X=0 and completes the cast — #123 verbatim, on a
surface whose fix never travelled, and on the one key the rest of the program
treats as "do nothing". The page even copies the CLI's refusal *string*
without the empty-input arm sitting next to it.

## The rest

**#560** — the residue of V44. `progress_fingerprint` hashes six of
`GameObject`'s forty fields, so `counters`, `attached_to`, `is_transformed`
and `power`/`toughness` move invisibly, as does the mulligan bookkeeping, and
the engine can ask a *different question* and score it as no progress (643
times in 52,837 decisions). A werewolf flipping is the one a player can see.
Filed as latent and labelled so: for each field the reachability question was
asked and answered no, because every `ManaCost::free()` ability in
`cards/isd/` also taps or sacrifices and both of those are hashed. It is a
claim about the next card — a level-up, a monstrosity, any repeatable ability
whose whole effect is a counter — not about this pool.

**#566** — the CLI has a spinner for an LLM seat thinking and nothing for the
engine thinking. Filed explicitly as a UX judgment, with one hard consequence
that is not a judgment: keystrokes typed into the dead window are buffered and
answer the *next* prompts unseen, and three fast Enters at a 500-permanent
board passed priority through `BeginCombat` and landed on `DECLARE ATTACKERS`
with neither screen read.

**#562, #563** — the refusal half of V46. Four styles across nine readers, and
two prompts (the deck browser and the battlefield inspector) that refuse
*silently*, redrawing a byte-identical page for `99` and for `zzz`. On the
bottoming screen `0 7`, `007` and `0,7` come back as three identical
refusals, which matters because #322 deliberately makes `0<Tab>7` and `07`
different inputs.

**#564** — `random.rs` answers every `ChooseXFunding` with
pool-plus-`max_contribution()`, which is `max_x` by construction. Over 8
mirrors and 55 casts, 36 were X=0 and every other one was the board maximum:
no seeded game has ever announced an intermediate X. That is CLAUDE.md's
second rule — never let a non-interactive seat answer with a constant —
sitting directly above the three `random.rs` arms already fixed for it
(#455/#456/#457).

## One note on the tooling

None of the eight issue **bodies** carries the attribution footer, and it is
not because anybody omitted it. Re-sending a body verbatim with the footer
appended is a no-op — `updated_at` comes back unchanged, identical to
`created_at` — so the footer is being stripped before the comparison. The
discriminating test was a comment with the footer placed deliberately in the
middle of the text: it was stored with the footer *moved to the end*. So the
two paths normalize differently — comments keep the footer and force it last,
issue bodies drop it entirely. Attribution comments were posted on the four
V46 issues instead. Worth knowing, because the same strip will silently hit
every future `phase:playtest`, `phase:fuzz` and `phase:mutants` filing and is
invisible unless someone reads the issue back after posting.

## What the guides gained

Four ideas added, all traceable to something observed tonight: **V47** (read
the two runner game loops side by side as one protocol, from #559 being the
third instance of that shape), **V48** (sweep every text-to-number reader in
the repo and compare what each *accepts*, from #561), **V49** (the X you
cannot buy, from reading `funding.rs` — four surfaces round an unfundable X
down in four different places and only one says so), and **V50** (measure the
per-decision budget on the other three surfaces, from V45's random-seat resume
proving the cost is not CLI-specific).
