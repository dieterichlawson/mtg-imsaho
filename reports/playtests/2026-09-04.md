# Playtest night — 2026-09-04

Crew of eight testers, sixteen missions, thirty issues filed (#193–#222).

## What we picked, and why

`reports/playtests/LEDGER.md` had five nights in it and every one of them
was spent on two subjects: the game (C, L) and the CLI (V). Two subjects
had **never been played at all** — the harness (H) and drafting (D) — and
inside the CLI subject the whole Operator series (M) was untouched. The
README says to prefer the subject that has gone longest without a night
and, inside it, ideas nobody has tried. That decided the night almost
entirely on its own.

It also meant the game and CLI subjects had nothing legal left: every C,
L and V idea in the guides was played between 2026-08-29 and 2026-09-02,
all inside the two-week floor. So the two game missions tonight are new
ideas, written before they were played and added to `playing.md` below.

## The night in one sentence per subject

**The harness is accurate about the game and careless about the process.**
Nothing leaks — hidden entries are `Private` and filtered from both the
prompt and the resume recap — and across ~60 decisions the actions offered
were exactly the actions the engine accepted, with zero malformed answers
and zero retries. H4 was the night's cleanest pass: after a `kill -9` at
turn 13, the resumed seat's recap matched the pre-kill conversation 130
entries out of 130, and its first prompt was byte-identical to the one it
had been looking at. What is broken is everything around the subprocess:
the 300-second call timeout does not actually unblock the game (#203), a
killed run leaks its `claude -p` process tree (#206), and two different
"we couldn't understand the seat" paths quietly play the game for it
(#209, #211).

**Drafting fails politely, which is worse than failing.** Three separate
probes ended in a run that exited 0, printed standings, and had done
nothing of the kind: 84 picks that were all card 0 with no log line
anywhere (#195), and a "deck" that was the undrafted pool with 17
wrong-coloured lands stapled on, ranked by the tournament as legitimate
(#200). The collation itself is genuinely faithful — 155,000 packs with
zero sequential-adjacency violations, C1 variants at 60.0/40.0 — except
the foil slot is inverted (#204) and, the arithmetic find of the night,
`pack_index % 2` drives both the C1/C2 choice and the rare sheet, so in
any even-sized pod half the seats are structurally incapable of opening
a mythic (#202). Observed: `[6, 0, 3, 0, 5, 0, 3, 0]`.

**The CLI keeps its data promises and breaks its documentation
promises.** M1 and M2 both came back clean in the way that matters: four
kill-and-resume cycles at four kinds of prompt produced a byte-identical
screen every time, and 58 turns of life, draws, damage and library counts
reconciled from the log alone to the last frame with zero mismatches.
Then three bugs with one shape — the runner tells the operator something
about a flag that isn't true. `--log` says it appends and truncates
(#194); `--resume` says `--seed` is ignored while quietly using it
(#196); `--resume` says `--deck1` is ignored and then dies because of it
(#197); and `--on-the-play`, the one flag genuinely ignored on resume, is
the one the note never mentions.

**The engine, again, is the healthy part.** Both game missions set out to
break a rules area and failed to. C24 checked nine CR claims about
planeswalkers in combat and all nine held, including the two we most
expected to be wrong: a walker hitting the graveyard on a state-based
check *while its own loyalty ability was still on the stack* (which then
resolved correctly), and trample assigning exactly lethal-measured-in-
loyalty before spilling to the controller. L27 put an opponent's
reanimated creature under our control wearing one player's Aura and the
other's Equipment, killed it, and watched all three go exactly where CR
404.3 / 704.5m / 704.5n say they go. All three defects from C24 and the
one from L27 are a layer out from the rules — rendering, fuzz coverage,
and a zone with no order.

## Notable moments

- **The hang that outlived its own watchdog.** At T+300s the `claude -p`
  timeout fired exactly as designed, the child went to `Z` — and the game
  sat there anyway, because the harness waits for EOF on a pipe the
  killed process's own child still held open. Observed 8m36s on one
  decision, roughly 2× the timeout, with a pristine log: no error, no
  retry, nothing to tell an unattended overnight run it had died
  standing up (#203).
- **A stub that cancels every concede still conceded four games out of
  four.** `pick_action_index` takes an index into the *displayed* option
  list and tests the concede guard against `legal_actions`, so the guard
  that exists to stop a garbled seat throwing the game is present or
  absent depending on whether two identical Grizzly Bears happened to
  collapse into one option that turn (#209).
- **A fix applied to one renderer and not the other.** `cli.rs` carries a
  comment citing CR 702.5c and issue #81 — "two curses on opposite
  players rendered identically" — and the identical defect is still live
  in the LLM prompt, which never says which player a Curse enchants
  (#198). `back_face_data()` has the same shape: fully populated, two
  files from a card reference that never asks for it, so a seat is asked
  "transform?" without being told what it becomes (#205).
- **A full `/tmp` panics a game that never mentioned `--save`.** The
  hot-reload snapshot is written on every decision of every `cli` game
  and does `expect("Failed to write hot-reload save")` — exit 101 with a
  backtrace — three lines above a sibling that handles the identical
  `StorageFull` cleanly, as #69 promised (#214).
- **The fuzzer's blind spot.** `RandomPlayer::choose_combat` throws away
  `defending_planeswalkers` and hard-codes `planeswalker_attacks:
  vec![]`. Across 30 seeded games with 464 Lilianas resolved and 303
  attack declarations, the random seat attacked a planeswalker zero
  times, so the invariants guarding that path have never once fired
  (#220). The guide's promise that the fuzzer already covers what is in
  `invariants/` holds only for actions the random seat can produce.
- **Money never leaked.** D7 verified the thing that would actually
  cost: `strace -e trace=connect,socket` across a complete all-`cc` draft
  recorded zero `socket()` and zero `connect()` syscalls.
