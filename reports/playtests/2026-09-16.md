# Playtest night — 2026-09-16

**Subject**: the CLI (the machine), chosen from the ledger rather than from
memory. Deriving each subject's last night out of `reports/playtests/LEDGER.md`
gave the game (C/L) 09-15, the harness (H) 09-14, drafting (D) 09-13 and the
CLI (V/M) 09-12 — so the CLI had gone longest without one. Reading the ids
actually run against the ids in `docs/playtest/cli.md` showed **every** CLI idea
has been run at least once (V1–V43, M1–M5), so there were no untried ideas to
take first. The oldest were V1, V2, V3, V4 and V7, all last played 2026-08-29
and so eighteen days past the two-week floor. **V7** was taken as the headline
because the week's five commits (#501–#506) had all added text to the very rows
V7 measures; **V4** and **V2** ran in parallel as subagents on their own tmux
sockets (`v4`, `v2`) and their own `logs/playtest/` prefixes.

**Crew**: 3 probes. **V7** run here; **V4** and **V2** in parallel.

**Runs**: 17 games through the real `mtg-runner` as hotseat `cli`-vs-`cli`
self-play, plus `random`-vs-`random` runs for the watchdog repro, a 14-width
rendering sweep, 5 cold-start renders and 5 refused resumes. Every game under
`--check-invariants`; **no invariant fired in any of them**, and there were no
panics and no hangs. **No metered API seat was spawned at any point** — every
seat in the night was `cli` or `random`; no `claude`, `cc` or `gemini` seat was
used at all.

**Issues**: 6 filed — #507, #508, #509, #510, #511, #512.

The night's shape: **the program's logic is right and its output is not.** Three
probes went at three unrelated parts of the machine — pane rendering, degenerate
boards, numbered-menu input — and all three found the behaviour underneath
correct on every claim they could reach. No wrong number was accepted, no board
however degenerate broke a rule, no index ever selected the wrong object. All
six issues are about what the program *shows* or what it *counts*: rows printed
past their pane, a flag clipped off, a fact elided, a screen that stopped
printing ids, and a watchdog that cannot see the decision it is judging.

## The three that matter

**#509 — the progress watchdog kills healthy games, and forfeits matches.**
This is the night's most consequential find and it came out of V4, which was
not looking for it. `progress_fingerprint` (`mtg-player/src/watchdog.rs:38`)
decides whether a game is advancing by hashing a hand-written list of fields:
turn, step, stack length, per-player totals, and per-object zone, controller,
tapped, summoning_sick, damage_marked. It hashes **nothing from `state.combat`**.
`place_in_damage_assignment_order` (`mtg-engine/src/combat.rs:224`) writes
**only** `combat.damage_assignment_order`. So every single
`ChooseDamageAssignmentOrder` decision leaves the fingerprint byte-identical,
and `STALLED_DECISIONS = 100` of them in a row ends the run with exit 1 and a
diagnosis — "answering it the same unusable way every time" — that is exactly
backwards. A token-flood mirror reaches it on demand: 42 + 49 + 10 accepted
placements across three attackers, `grep -c "choice refused"` on the DEBUG log
returning **0**, and 7.8 seconds between the two genuinely completed orders.
Reproduced 3/3 on seed 7, and verified in the source from both ends before
being written up. The tournament loop shares the watchdog and forfeits the game
rather than stopping it, so the same false positive costs a match there. The
general shape is now **V44**: a watchdog whose fingerprint is an explicit field
list scores any decision moving state outside that list as a stall, and
degenerate decks are the cheapest way to make one prompt repeat a hundred times.

**#507 — two of five permanent categories are never clipped to the pane.**
Found by reading `render_battlefield_at` before playing anything. The creature
and planeswalker rows end in `.chars().take(max_w)` and `render_lands` spends
its budget entry by entry and degrades to `+N more` — but the enchantment row
(`cli.rs:2137`) and the artifact row (`cli.rs:2150`) are a bare
`Print(counted_line(n, &label))` with no clip at all. That is #244's and #350's
exact failure surviving on the two categories nobody had built a wide board
for, and the enchantment label is the one that has been *growing*: it is now
name + legend mark + `[enchanting you|opponent]` + `[names: X]` + counters. A
**single** Curse of the Pierced Heart — the pool's longest card name at 26
characters — is a 50-column row needing no `Nx` prefix, and it bleeds 3 columns
into the STACK/LOG pane at 60 wide and 17 at 32, where it also erases the
`· · ·` board separator. The contrast that makes it a clipping omission rather
than a too-narrow terminal is on the same screen every time: the `Lands:` line,
both life lines and the HAND rows all clip correctly beside it.

**#512 — the screen that replaced the menus never inherited their rules.**
`draw_set_screen` (`cli.rs:6239`) wraps `pick.rows` directly and never calls
`menu_row_texts` (`cli.rs:3382`), which is where #136/#257/#258's rule lives:
append the distinguishing `(#id)` when two rows would render identically. None
of its five callers puts an id in the string, and `perm_name` deliberately
*stopped* carrying one when #258 moved it onto `MenuLabel` — so on this screen
the id is not clipped, it was never added. "Mark up to 2 of the 7 targets" over
seven Avacyn's Pilgrims is seven indistinguishable rows; the same two objects
render as `(#26)` and `(#41)` on the combat screen sixty seconds later. The
failure mode is worth naming because it has bitten before: a surface that
*replaces* another does not inherit the other's fixes, which is #404 (a second
request path missing a fix) in a different place.

## The other three

**#508** — `elide_middle` reserves 4 columns for the `Nx ` prefix while
`counted_line` actually spends 5 (`"  " + "2x "`) or 6 (`"10x "`). Every
collapsed creature row is therefore one or two columns over `max_w`, and the
`take(max_w)` behind it eats the tail — which is `flags`, the region #270 moved
last precisely so a long attachment list could not push it off. It renders as
`2x Elite Inquisitor 2/2 (first… [S`, and unlike the `n == 1` path there is no
`…` to say anything was dropped. Severity is bounded by `i`, which lists every
permanent uncollapsed and unelided; a correction saying so was posted to the
issue after the fact.

**#510** — #504's restrictions are appended after `legendary`, every keyword and
every protection, inside `elide_middle`'s *elastic* region, which is cut from
the tail. So `can't block this turn` is the first thing elided: absent at
120x45 (the house default) and at 200, present at 300. The priority is
inverted — `legendary` and `flying` are stable printed-card facts readable off
the CARDS pane at leisure, while the restriction is volatile, lasts one turn, is
on no card, and decides whether a block is legal. The LLM board helper has no
width budget at all, so a model seat is told `can't block this turn` on every
call while the person at the keyboard is not.

**#511** — the damage-assignment-order prompt carries two 14-space runs from a
collapsed multi-line literal (`mtg-engine/src/combat.rs:205`), printed verbatim
on the CLI takeover screen, sent verbatim to an LLM seat, and echoed in the
watchdog's stderr. The same string also still says "first", the pre-#325
one-at-a-time wording, directly above a footer offering to keep the whole order.
Filed low.

## What held, and is worth not re-finding

- **Every numbered reader.** Thirteen prompt kinds against `-1`, `-0`, `N+1`,
  `2^31`, `2^63`, `2^64`, `3.0`, `3,4`, repeated indices, empty submits and
  fullwidth digits: no wrong number silently accepted as a different legal
  action, no clamping, no saturation, no partial commit, no legal index
  refused. Verified against `--log` and `--save` rather than the screen,
  including from pages where the chosen row was not drawn — so **V26's
  index-accuracy question is now answered off-page on four prompt kinds**. The
  only scale left is #471's 6402-row `ActivateAbility` menu.
- **Degenerate boards.** Zero creatures on both seats gives 20 BeginCombat and
  20 DeclareAttackers steps with 0 DeclareBlockers and 0 CombatDamage
  (CR 506.5) and no prompt ever drawn. Claustrophobia sat in hand all game with
  nine untapped lands and was never offered, having no legal target
  (CR 601.2c). The legend rule fired **30 times** in one 108-turn game across
  both seats and refused all 8 hostile inputs with `--save` and `--log`
  md5-identical across all 9 attempts. A 2,735-token board still collapsed to
  `683x Zombie 2/2 [tok]`, paged its 2,772-row inspector, and stayed
  index-accurate at an off-page index.
- **Rendering everywhere else.** Swept at 200, 120, 110, 105, 100, 80, 70, 60,
  50, 46, 45, 40, 32 and 28 columns: the `Lands:` line, both life lines, the
  HAND rows, the STACK and LOG panes, and the creature and planeswalker rows
  all clip or wrap correctly at every width.
- **Three fixes confirmed at runtime, not read.** #328/#351/#352 — the
  declare-attackers list now wraps inside the pane with a hanging indent at 100
  columns and still shows its creature list at 70x20. #316 — `--resume` refuses
  a finished save with a clean error naming the winner and the action count.
- **Viewers do not leak input.** A digit typed inside the `l` viewer closes it
  and does not fall through to the menu underneath.

## Checked and deliberately not filed

- The frame's right border is not drawn below the CARDS pane's content. Uniform
  at 100, 105, 110 and 120 columns and on cold starts as well as after a
  resize — consistent behaviour, not a break.
- `showing A-B of C-D` is index notation, not an off-by-one; `cli.rs:1403`
  formats it in one place because the indices are absolute across pages.
- The lenient reader (`+3` and `007` accepted, `-0` refused) always selects the
  value it names, so it is leniency rather than mis-selection. Recorded as
  **V46** instead, together with the four readers that disagree on whether a
  refusal quotes the input or the parsed value.
- 80 seconds per keypress at 2,735 permanents, with the screen lagging several
  seconds behind input so a live game looks hung. It always finished and never
  wedged; recorded as **V45** with a method for finding where it stops being
  playable, which nobody has located.
