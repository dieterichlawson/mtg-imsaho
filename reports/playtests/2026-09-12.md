## V43 — does a viewer ever paint in raw mode?

**Result: the program's own prompts are clean. The 2026-09-11 sighting is real,
and its trigger is the terminal the program is handed, not a prompt inside it.**

The idea existed because a 2026-09-11 L44 probe reported every row of `s`, `g`
and `l` cascading right — each line starting where the previous one ended, the
LF-without-CR signature, with `(empty)` landing at column 9 — and the lead could
not reproduce it. The idea named the suspects: `show_paged_lines` writes every
row as `Print(format!("{s}\n"))`, a bare LF that only renders as a newline
because the tty's cooked line discipline turns it into CRLF, so *some* prompt
must be handing a viewer a terminal that is still raw.

**No prompt is.** Reading first — `read_line` → `read_line_redrawing`
(`tui_raw_on` at `cli.rs:3623`, `tui_raw_off` at `3680`),
`read_line_with_search_redrawing` (`3775`/`3873`), `run_card_search`
(`2553`/`2610`), `library_search_ui` (`6270`, which binds no viewer key anyway)
— every reader in the file is raw-balanced, and the one asymmetric function,
`confirm_yn` (`3698`, which deliberately leaves raw on when it was already on),
is reached from exactly one call site where it was off. Then playing it: all six
viewers (`l` `g` `e` `s` `i` `d`) opened from seven prompt kinds — the mulligan
decision, the `pick_set` bottom-N marking screen, the target chooser, the
priority action menu, declare attackers, declare blockers and the trigger-ordering
screen — **42 opens, every one rendered correctly**. `C-z` then `fg` at a prompt
and then a viewer is clean too.

**What does reproduce it is a terminal whose `OPOST` was already off when the
process started** (#470). One `stty raw -echo` in front of the binary is enough:

```
 STACK

        (empty)
                 Press enter to return...
```

`(empty)` at column 9 — `" STACK"` is 6 columns, the LF leaves the cursor there,
the row's own two-space indent puts the `(` at column 9. That is the 2026-09-11
measurement, exactly.

The program cannot recover on its own, which is the part worth knowing:
`tui_raw_off()` calls crossterm's `disable_raw_mode`, which restores the termios
crossterm snapshotted at its *first* `enable_raw_mode`. If the terminal was
already raw then, that snapshot *is* the raw termios, so cooked mode never comes
back for the life of the process.

Three further facts that bound it:

- **Only the viewers.** The ordinary frame is fine on the same terminal, because
  `render_paged` positions with `cursor::MoveTo`. So do the two newest
  full-screen prompts, `draw_ordering_screen` and `draw_set_screen`, which
  `MoveTo(0, *row)` each row and were verified clean on the raw terminal.
  `library_search_ui` and both line readers write `"\n\r"` / `"\r\n"`
  explicitly. There are three idioms in `cli.rs` for ending a row and only the
  six viewers use the one that is terminal-dependent.
- **The page arithmetic then overflows.** `show_paged_lines` sizes a page with
  `wrapped_height(..., w)`, which assumes each row starts at column 0. Under the
  cascade rows start further right each time and wrap where the arithmetic says
  they do not, so the page is taller than the screen and the
  `(showing X-Y of Z)` heading — the only thing saying which slice you are
  looking at — scrolls off the top. Reproduced with a 138-entry log at 100x30.
- **The program does not create the precondition for itself under an
  interactive shell.** `opost` was measured before, during and after a game
  exited by SIGKILL, SIGTERM, SIGHUP and Ctrl-C in a pane running bash: restored
  every time, because bash resets the terminal when it regains control. The
  realistic paths are a harness or wrapper that raw-modes the pty, `script`-style
  drivers, and a predecessor killed in a pane with no shell behind it.

**What came back clean and is worth not re-finding.** #365's pager fix holds
exactly: the log viewer was walked at 120x45, 100x30, 80x24, 70x20, 45x18,
30x12, 24x10 and 20x8, and at every size the page fills the screen to the row,
the wrapped heading and wrapped footer are both counted, `p` from the opening
(`start_at_end`) window reaches entry 1 and `n` reaches the last entry.

## V42 — how big can a question get?

**Result: every `CastSpell` shape held. The blowup that is left is in the
`ActivateAbility` cost, which is the half `prompt_shapes.rs` structurally cannot
see.**

Full detail is in #471 and #472. The short form:

- **Held, with room to spare.** A sweep of every card in the pool on one wide
  board (8 creatures, 8 lands and 16 graveyard cards a side) tops out at 32 cast
  actions, and the CLI collapses all of them through `legal.castable_spells` to
  one menu row. Into the Maw of Hell over 16 lands and 16 creatures,
  Ghoulcaller's Chant over a 12-Zombie graveyard, Prey Upon at 8v8, Harvest Pyre
  over a 40-card graveyard and Curse of Oblivion are each a single row.
  `pick_set`, `prompt_ordering` and the blocker prompt are all one row per
  object. The conversion V42 was sent to check did hold.
- **Broke (#471).** An activated ability with both a target *and* a chooseable
  sacrifice is enumerated one action per `(target, sacrifice)` pair, and
  `build_action_menu` pushes a row for every one. Demonmail Hauberk is 25 menu
  rows at five creatures (verified on screen), 50 with a second copy, 144 at
  twelve, and 6402 at four Hauberks and forty creatures; Skirsdag Cultist is 171
  at eight creatures a side. Exactly two abilities in the pool have that shape
  and both blow up. The blowup is in the cost, not in `target_requirement`,
  which is why the test sweep cannot see it.
- **The direction is the surprise (#472).** V42 predicted the LLM side would be
  worse. It is the opposite: `format_action_prompt` is newline-joined, and
  `choose_action` dedupes `ActivateAbility` by (object, ability index), logging
  `COLLAPSED 173 actions → 3 options` and then asking target and sacrifice a
  slot at a time. The CLI is the only readable surface carrying the product. The
  third surface is worse again — `random.rs` draws uniformly over
  `legal.actions`, so 64 of 65 non-concede slots are one equip and the fuzzer
  takes it 98.5% of the time, crowding combat and casting out of the
  distribution. That is the same family as #455/#456/#457, inverted: not a seat
  answering with a constant, but a seat whose uniform draw is over encodings
  rather than over decisions.
