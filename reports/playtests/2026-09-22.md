## The method the night is worth keeping for

Every probe measured instead of looking. A Playwright `addInitScript` wraps
`CanvasRenderingContext2D.prototype.fillText` before the page's own scripts
run, so every string the page paints is recorded with its `measureText` width,
its font and its alignment:

```js
await page.addInitScript(() => {
  window.__draws = [];
  const orig = CanvasRenderingContext2D.prototype.fillText;
  CanvasRenderingContext2D.prototype.fillText = function (s, x, y) {
    window.__draws.push({ s: String(s), x, y, w: this.measureText(String(s)).width, font: this.font });
    return orig.apply(this, arguments);
  };
});
```

"Does it fit" then becomes arithmetic on `x + w` against 640 and against
`PANEL_X = 480`, in the same spirit as G4's `window.mtg.hits` rule from
09-17 — and unlike `hits`, it covers text the page draws without publishing a
rectangle for it, which is most of it. Pair it with `window.mtgDebug.render()`
to take a frame on demand and with `m.hover = <hit>` to point the inspector at
anything on the board, and one game yields a few hundred measured frames.

## G14 — the unclipped-text sweep (#568)

Two halves, and they answered in opposite directions.

**In ordinary play the page fits.** Two complete games (seeds 11 and 12,
innistrad-white vs blue-white and red-green vs rg-coverage, 99 decisions), with
the inspector driven over every permanent, hand card, stack chip and player
strip at every fourth decision — 542 hover frames — plus the log drawer and all
three zone overlays opened every twentieth decision. **Zero** strings left
their pane. #522 and #532 took the ones that used to.

**At the longest string it can hold, one site does not.** `inspector()` draws
the P/T line with a bare `text()` into an 84px box at x=552, through neither
`clip()` nor `wrap()`:

| string | width | right edge |
|---|---|---|
| `13/13` | 40px | 592 — fits |
| `13/13 5 dmg` | 88px | 640 — overruns the box, flush to the canvas edge |
| `13/13 10 dmg` | 96px | **648 — 8px past the canvas** |

Ludevic's Abomination is a real 13/13 with 13 toughness, so 10–12 damage marked
is a board state the engine can produce, and the player is shown
`13/13 10 dm`. The row directly above it — the card name — ellipsizes correctly
at 632, because *it* goes through `wrapCapped` → `wrap` → `clip`, which is
where #532 put the rule. The second P/T line, `(printed 13/13)`, is 90px in the
same 84px box.

Worth noting for the other three surfaces: this is a pure page bug. The CLI's
`i` screen and the LLM seat's card reference both state P/T as text with no
fixed-width box, so neither can lose a character this way.

## G15 — the window that is too small (#569)

Proposed 09-17 as a prediction from reading `index.html` and `fitCanvas`.
Confirmed, and worse at the top of the range than the prediction suggested.

`fitCanvas` floors the integer scale at 1 and `body { overflow: hidden }`
removes the browser's own fallback, so below 640x360 the canvas is centred and
cut on both sides with no scrollbar and nothing on screen saying so. The half
that goes first is the right-hand 160px, which is the entire prompt.

| viewport | canvas on screen | prompt panel visible | buttons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 620x700 (half a laptop screen) | 97% | 150px of 160 | clickable |
| 500x700 | 78% | 90 of 160 | clickable |
| 390x844 (phone portrait) | 61% | **35 of 160** | **both off-screen, clicking does nothing** |
| 320x240 | 33% | **0 of 160** | **both off-screen, clicking does nothing** |

The keyboard is a partial way out and only partial: `keydown` is on `window`,
so Enter still passes at a priority prompt at 320x240 — but every decision that
must be clicked has no keyboard equivalent, and (see G16) Enter is dead at the
mulligan, which is the first decision of the game.

## G16 — the destructive default, for the widgets nobody had asked (#570)

#520, #524, #561 and #518 between them taught three widgets what to do when
the player presses Enter having touched nothing. Tonight asked the other two.

| widget | Enter | is the player told? |
|---|---|---|
| `menu` | sends `PassPriority` | n/a |
| `order` | confirms | n/a (#518) |
| `number` | refuses | yes — `"Enter a value for X."` (#561) |
| `mark` (min 1) | refuses | yes — `"0 marked — mark between 1 and 2 cards"` |
| **`pick`** | **nothing** | **no** |
| **`list`** | **nothing** | **no** |

`main.ts:322` tries `canPass`, then `onConfirm`, then `number`'s `submit`, then
`preventDefault`s the key and draws an identical frame. `pick` and `list` set
none of the three. `list` is the mulligan: the first thing the page ever asks,
where Enter — the key `mtg-gui/README.md` documents as "passes or confirms" —
does nothing and says nothing. Escape is the same on both.

The `mark` row is what makes it a defect rather than a design choice. On a
widget that also has no default answer, the same key produces a sentence.
Doing nothing here is probably right; the silence is not.

## G23 — the inspector has no ceiling: **negative, don't re-run it as written**

The idea predicted that a fact-heavy permanent pushes the prompt pane down far
enough to delete prompt rows "and, far enough, the Pass button". **The buttons
never move off the canvas, and the arithmetic says they cannot.**
`promptH = H - promptY - 4` with the log closed, so `promptY + promptH` is
`H - 4` whatever the inspector does, and the buttons are laid out upward from
`y + h - 18`. They were at y=323/338 at every fact count from 0 to 17.

With the log open (`l`) `promptH` is pinned at 100 and the buttons *do* drift —
y=261 at one fact, y=287 at seventeen — but that is ~8px per extra fact, so
reaching the bottom edge needs about 23 facts.

What the sweep did find is a real but currently unreachable starvation. The
inspector's `facts` are uncapped and the card's own rules text is whatever is
left: `maxLines = Math.floor((y + 200 - ty) / 9)`.

| facts | rules-text lines drawn |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | 10 (all of it) |
| 9 | 7 |
| 12 | 4 |
| 13 | 2 |
| 14 | 1 |
| **15+** | **0 — and silently, because `wrapCapped` only marks the cut on a line it keeps** |

So between 7 and 14 facts the text is truncated with an ellipsis, and at 15 it
vanishes with no mark at all — on the page's only surface for reading what a
card does. But **a real game did not get near it**: a purpose-built
Elite Inquisitor deck (14 Plains / 4 Forest / 12 Elite Inquisitor / 6 Travel
Preparations / 4 Cobbled Wings) played to completion against a zombie deck
peaked at **6 facts** — Color, the keyword line, three protections and
Summoning sick — with all 4 of its rules-text lines still drawn. Nothing was
ever equipped or enchanted in that run, which is the gap worth closing before
anyone files this: see the wrinkle added to G23 in the guide.
