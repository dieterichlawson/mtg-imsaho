## L43 — the intervening "if" that is still true when the target has gone

**Result: no bug. Every check held, and the third state turned out to be
reachable and distinguishable.**

L42 had established both halves of CR 603.4 but never reached the interaction
with CR 608.2b: what happens when the condition is *still true* and the target
is gone. Only two implemented cards have an intervening "if" *and* a target, and
both were reached.

**Morkrut Banshee.** Morbid on from a combat death (Typhoid Rats traded with a
Walking Corpse), Banshee cast post-combat, its ETB trigger given the Banshee
itself as its target, then Victim of Night cast on the Banshee in response:

```
p0's Morkrut Banshee (#22)'s ETB trigger (if morbid, target creature gets -4/-4 until end of turn) goes on the stack targeting Morkrut Banshee (#22)
p0 cast Victim of Night (#31) targeting Morkrut Banshee (#22)
Victim of Night (#31) resolved
Morkrut Banshee (#22) died
Morkrut Banshee (#22)'s ETB trigger (if morbid, target creature gets -4/-4 until end of turn) fizzled (all targets illegal)
```

Nothing took -4/-4 — the opponent's Walking Corpse and Diregraf Ghoul were both
still 2/2 on the next screen.

**Reaper from the Abyss.** Morbid on from blocking a Walking Corpse with the
Reaper, the end-step trigger targeting p0's own Typhoid Rats, then Altar's Reap
sacrificing that Rats in response:

```
p0's Reaper from the Abyss (#21)'s end step trigger (if morbid, destroy target non-Demon creature) goes on the stack targeting Typhoid Rats (#35)
p0 sacrificed Typhoid Rats (#35) as an additional cost of Altar's Reap (#26)
Typhoid Rats (#35) died
Reaper from the Abyss (#21)'s end step trigger (if morbid, destroy target non-Demon creature) fizzled (all targets illegal)
```

The opponent's Diregraf Ghoul survived. Neither log line says anything about
morbid, which is what the probe was for: the comment in
`reaper_from_the_abyss.rs` records a regression where a Reaper that never
triggered reported "no legal targets", and the fix moved the morbid check into
`should_trigger`. The question this night could still ask was whether that fix
collapsed the *other* direction — whether "morbid true, nothing to target" had
become indistinguishable from "morbid false".

It has not. All three states are distinct in the log:

| state | what the log says |
|---|---|
| morbid false | **nothing at all** — 4 end steps with the Reaper on the battlefield produced zero trigger lines |
| morbid true, no legal target | `Trigger removed: no legal targets (Reaper from the Abyss (#21)'s end step trigger (if morbid, destroy target non-Demon creature))` |
| morbid true, target removed in response | `... fizzled (all targets illegal)` |

The middle row was reached by getting two Reapers onto the battlefield,
sacrificing one to Altar's Reap for morbid, and clearing the opponent's board
first — with only a Demon left, the trigger has morbid but nothing it may
target.

Two things for anyone repeating this. The deck budget L43 shipped with does not
work: Victim of Night is "non-Vampire, non-Werewolf, non-Zombie", so against a
Zombie deck it cannot remove any target the trigger might pick, and the response
has to be Altar's Reap sacrificing one of your own. And the target prompt only
appears when there is a real choice — with one legal target the engine locks it
in with no prompt, which looks exactly like an auto-choice if you are sending
keystrokes without reading the screen between them. Checking the stack pane
(`s`) settles it.

## L45 (new idea) — the destroy pipeline as a reporting contract

**Result: two issues, #467 and #468.**

L43's route into Reaper from the Abyss went through `PendingEffect::DestroyCreature`,
and reading that handler was the whole of this probe. `mtg-engine/src/destruction.rs`
returns a `DestroyResult` — `Died` / `Regenerated` / `Indestructible` /
`NotAPermanent` — and carries a `try_destroy_by` helper whose doc comment says
exactly why:

> five cards wrote their own "X destroyed Y" line beside it without looking at
> the result. Ghost Quarter's ruling is explicit that the land can survive …
> and the log said it was destroyed anyway. … the line that names the card is
> the one a reader trusts, so it has to be the true one.

Five cards were converted. The shared handler was not:

```rust
crate::destruction::try_destroy(state, *id, registry);
state.log(LogLevel::Event, format!("{source_name} destroyed {name}"));
```

The result is dropped and the claim is made either way. Two cards route through
it — Slayer of the Wicked and Reaper from the Abyss — and **both arms
reproduce**:

- Regeneration: a Walking Corpse wearing Skeletal Grimace (`{B}: Regenerate`),
  regenerated in response to Slayer of the Wicked's trigger →
  `Walking Corpse (#58) regenerated` immediately followed by
  `Slayer of the Wicked destroyed Walking Corpse (#58)`. Two adjacent lines
  that contradict each other.
- Indestructible: Manor Gargoyle (`{5}`, colourless, indestructible as long as
  it has defender) under Reaper from the Abyss's trigger →
  `Reaper from the Abyss destroyed Manor Gargoyle (#64)`, and that is the log's
  **only** statement about the event. The Gargoyle is on the battlefield on the
  next upkeep and the opponent's graveyard is empty.

The game state is right in every case — regeneration taps and saves, CR 701.15a;
indestructible refuses, CR 701.7b. Only the account of it is wrong. The audit
also cleared the neighbours: Witchbane Orb and Paraselene branch on the result
correctly, and Divine Reckoning makes no claim at all.

Two things raise this above cosmetic. The smaller one is ordering: the naming
line is written *after* `try_destroy`, so even when the creature genuinely dies
the log reports the consequence before the cause —
`Diregraf Ghoul (#78) died` precedes
`Reaper from the Abyss destroyed Diregraf Ghoul (#78)`.

The larger one is the second surface. `mtg-player/src/llm.rs` hands the engine's
log to an LLM seat twice over: every prompt carries the entries since the last
one, and `resume_from_log` sends the whole log as the seat's entire account of
the game it is rejoining. A CLI player sees the board contradict the line — the
renderer even prints `(defender, indestructible)` on the Gargoyle in the same
frame. A model has only the line.

**#468** came out of building that repro: a live regeneration shield is rendered
nowhere. `regeneration_shields` does not appear anywhere in `mtg-player/`, so
the battlefield line does not mark it, the `i` inspect list does not, and the
per-permanent detail screen — which lists type, colour, printed and effective
P/T, controller, tapped, id and auras — does not either. The log records a
shield only when it is *spent*. A shield is public information under CR 701.15a
and it decides whether removal is worth casting and whether a block trades, so
both players are making that decision blind.

## L44 — the first 603.4 check, card by card

**Result: one issue, #469. The audit the idea asked for is done and should not
be repeated.**

Run as a subagent on its own `l44` socket. Three hotseat games, both seats
`cli`, `--check-invariants` on, ~90 turns, zero invariant violations, zero
panics, zero hangs.

The idea's premise was stale in two ways. Homicidal Brute — "ungated as far as
L42 could tell" — has had a `should_trigger` gate for a while, and all twelve
werewolf DFCs route through one shared `helpers::werewolf_should_trigger`
(`grep -c werewolf_should_trigger` returns 1 for each of the twelve). So there
was no missing gate to find, and the night became runtime verification plus a
hunt for a gate whose *predicate* is wrong. It found one.

**What was verified correct**, which is most of the value here:

- **Homicidal Brute, six configurations.** Attacked this turn → nothing on the
  stack, nothing in the log, no transform back, on four separate turns. Did not
  attack → the trigger fires, taps and flips it back, with a real priority
  window (`RESPOND TO your Homicidal Brute's end step trigger (transform back if
  didn't attack)`). Attacked on a *previous* turn → the trigger still fires,
  because `attacked_this_turn` is `attacked_on_turn == Some(turn_number)`
  (`state.rs:3139`) rather than a flag someone has to remember to clear.
  "Your end step" is the controller's (CR 603.2) — a Brute held untapped through
  the opponent's end step produced nothing. A Brute stolen by Traitorous Blood
  *after* attacking triggers for the thief, on the thief's end step, and the CLI
  titles the window `RESPOND TO p1's Homicidal Brute's end step trigger`; a Brute
  stolen and then attacked with produces nothing, because the stamp is on the
  object and not on the original controller. The front face declares no trigger
  at all.
- **All three morbid ETBs with the condition false** — Woodland Sleuth, Morkrut
  Banshee, Hollowhenge Scavenger. Each logs its resolve line and nothing else,
  `s` shows `(empty)`, and the Banshee offers **no target prompt at all**, which
  is exactly the CR 603.4-vs-603.3c distinction its doc comment argues for. The
  remaining morbid cards need no gate and have none: Somberwald Spider and
  Festerhide Boar are replacement effects, Skirsdag High Priest is an activation
  restriction, Caravan Vigil and Brimstone Volley are spell effects.
- **Both werewolf faces in both directions**, including the one-spell limbo that
  neither face wants (`sum == 0` false, `any >= 2` false → nothing triggers), and
  CR 603.3b ordering when two back-face werewolves trigger together.

**#469** is the one defect, and it is not a missing gate:

```rust
if *kind == TriggerKind::Upkeep {
    // A token copy of a werewolf cannot transform, so its transform
    // ability has nothing to do and should not reach the stack at all.
    if state.get_object(self_id).is_some_and(|o| o.is_token) {
        return false;
    }
    return behavior.should_transform(state, self_id, registry);
}
```

CR 603.4 tests the printed clause — "if a player cast two or more spells last
turn" — and nothing else. That a token cannot transform is CR 701.28c, a fact
about resolution, and `apply_transform` already refuses it correctly at
`helpers.rs:620`. So a Cackling Counterpart token copy of a Gatstaf Howler never
puts its trigger on the stack: with two identical Howlers out and two spells cast
last turn, one trigger fires and no ordering prompt appears.

The clinching argument is that the engine already disagrees with itself.
`apply_transform` refuses *two* kinds of permanent that cannot transform — tokens,
and single-faced clones via `copy_grantor` — and the gate knows about only one.
An Evil Twin copying a werewolf is not a token, so it triggers and then does
nothing on resolution, which is correct; the token of the same werewolf does not
trigger at all. Two permanents identical in the only respect the gate claims to
care about, treated differently, and the one it does not special-case is the one
it gets right.

Three configurations are **unreachable** in this card pool and are recorded in
the guide so nobody spends another night on them: a Brute attacked and stolen in
the same turn (Traitorous Blood is a sorcery, so the thief can never act after
the owner's declare-attackers step), attacking as Civilized Scholar and then
transforming (the `{T}` ability cannot be activated after the Scholar taps to
attack — only transform-then-attack is buildable, and the unit test covers the
ruling), and a Brute removed from combat after attacking (nothing in ISD removes
an attacker without moving it to another zone, and tapping an attacker does not
remove it from combat, CR 506.4).

## The one thing this night did not file

The L44 probe also reported that the full-screen viewers cascade right — every
row starting at the column where the previous one ended, the LF-without-CR
signature, with `(empty)` measured at column 9 = `len(" STACK")` plus the row's
own indent, and a game-log page wrapping mid-word off the right edge.

It does not reproduce for the lead. `s`, `g`, `e`, `l` and `i` all render
correctly at 200 columns and at 100, from the action menu, from a target menu
and from the post-mulligan bottoming screen. So it is not filed.

Both halves are worth keeping, because they are compatible. The cascade was
measured, not guessed — the column arithmetic is exact. And the code makes it
possible: `show_paged_lines` (`cli.rs:4117`) writes every row as
`Print(format!("{s}\n"))`, a bare line feed that only renders correctly because
the tty's cooked line discipline turns it into CRLF. Raw mode being off during
the paint is a coincidence of which reader last ran, not an invariant — and
`cli.rs:3670` has a reader that deliberately leaves raw mode on
(`if !was_raw { tui_raw_off(); }`) while `run_card_search` holds it across its
whole loop. That is scheduled as **V43** in `cli.md`: sweep every prompt that
advertises a viewer key, open all five from each, and find which one leaves raw
mode on.

## Housekeeping

Four games played here, three by the L44 probe, all `--p1 cli --p2 cli` under
`--check-invariants`. Zero invariant violations, zero panics, zero hangs, across
every game. Sockets `l43` and `l44` were separate throughout and neither crew
touched the default socket or the other's files; both are killed and both crews'
`logs/playtest/` files are deleted. One process slip worth recording: a helper
script invoked without its socket variables sent a keystroke into the wrong
game's window twice. Both times the game was one whose probe had already
finished, so no evidence was affected — but a driver script that defaults to a
socket rather than requiring one is a trap, and the next crew should make the
socket a required argument.
