# Playtest night — 2026-08-31

Three agents played the Competitor, Rules Lawyer and Vandal personas in
parallel, hotseat self-play through the real CLI, 10 games each (30 games
total, 15 missions). Every mission on the menu had been played on 2026-08-29
or 2026-08-30, so the whole menu was off-limits and **all 15 missions tonight
were new** (C10–C14, L11–L15, V11–V15); they are now in
`docs/plans/playtest-missions.md`. Per-mission detail is in
`reports/playtests/LEDGER.md`.

Every game ran with a distinct `--seed` (Competitor 1001–1010, Rules Lawyer
2001–2010, Vandal 3001–3010) so findings replay deterministically.

## Issues filed

**18 new issues**, all `bug` + `phase:playtest`: **#49–#66**. All open as of this
report. The previous night's issues (#45–#48) were all fixed and closed before
this run started.

Ranked by severity:

- **#64 — engine panic.** Rooftop Storm's alternative cost is applied to every
  card in a player's graveyard, so Zombie creature cards there are offered as
  `Flashback Diregraf Ghoul` — an action the rules never permit (CR 117.1a /
  601.3a; an alternative cost replaces a cost, it does not grant permission to
  cast from another zone). Choosing it panics at
  `mtg-engine/src/engine/actions/cast.rs:82` (`flashback cast on card without
  flashback_cost`) and kills the game mid-play. Root-caused to an unconditional
  `fb_costs.extend(alternative_costs(...))` in
  `mtg-engine/src/engine/legal/casting.rs:293`, which should be gated on the
  card already having a way to be cast from the graveyard. Found by the Rules
  Lawyer (L13) in a real game; the coordinator then reproduced it from scratch
  with a self-contained 40-card deck pair and a fixed seed, so the issue stays
  reproducible after tonight's logs are deleted. The process dies but the TUI is
  left frozen on its last frame, so it reads as a hang rather than a crash.
- **#62 — one creature can block two attackers** and deals its full power to
  *each* (CR 509.1b, CR 510.1c). A 2/1 Savannah Lions killed a 2/2 and a 3/1 in
  one combat. Per-pair block legality (flying, "can't be blocked by Humans") *is*
  enforced at both layers; nothing counts how many times a blocker id appears in
  the assignment list. Found by the Rules Lawyer (L12). Related to the #40 fix,
  which covered the other half of the same rule.
- **#50 — a single paste silently executes real game actions.** Queued lines are
  consumed as independent menu submissions, so one 30-line paste advanced the
  game 11 turns across both seats, including two cleanup discards. Nothing on
  screen indicates it happened. Found by the Vandal (V13).
- **#49 — casting an "up to N target" spell with no legal targets is a silent
  no-op.** The menu entry is offered, selecting it redraws the same menu, spends
  no mana and writes no log line, forever. CR 601.2c permits choosing zero
  targets. Found by the Vandal (V11, while just playing).
- **#65 — no damage-assignment-order choice** (CR 509.2). The lethal-in-order
  arithmetic is right, but the attacking player is never asked to order the
  blockers; the engine's internal iteration order decides. Found by the Rules
  Lawyer (L12).
- **#59 — Declare Blockers and Combat Damage steps still happen when no
  attackers were declared** (CR 508.8), handing out two extra priority windows.
  Currently deliberate — `mtg-engine/src/engine.rs:1174` says so in a comment.
  Found by the Competitor (C11).
- **#57 — cards in hand display P/T modified by battlefield anthems.**
  Layer 7c applies only to permanents (CR 613.1). The battlefield recomputes
  correctly at the same instant, so this is hand rendering only. Note for the
  fixer: characteristic-defining abilities *must* keep applying in hand
  (CR 604.3), and they currently do — the fix has to keep that. Found by the
  Rules Lawyer (L11).
- **#58 — planeswalkers are never drawn on the battlefield and loyalty is shown
  nowhere.** `render_battlefield_at` buckets permanents into lands / creatures /
  enchantments / artifacts only, so a planeswalker is never emitted; loyalty is
  in `PermanentView.counters` and rendered by the LLM player but not the CLI.
  Found by the Competitor (C10).
- **#52 — every bad `--resume` save panics** (exit 101), including a plain
  missing-file typo; two paths are raw `index out of bounds`. A schema-valid but
  structurally invalid save starts a silently wrong game unless
  `--check-invariants` is passed. Found by the Vandal (V14).
- **#51 — Ctrl-key chords are typed into the prompt as plain characters.**
  `Ctrl-L` opens the log, `Ctrl-F` engages auto-pass, `Ctrl-U` appends `u`
  instead of clearing, and `Ctrl-\ ] ^ _` type the digits `4 5 6 7` — observed
  selecting `6: Concede`. Only `Ctrl-C` is special-cased. Found by the Vandal
  (V12).
- **#56 — X-cost and graveyard-exile prompts are printed with raw `println!`
  over the TUI**, so the previous frame bleeds through mid-sentence and a stale
  menu stays on screen. The X prompt's empty-input default is X = 0, which
  silently killed a Mikaeus. Found independently by the Rules Lawyer (L11) and
  the Competitor (C10) on two different prompts; filed as one issue since both
  are the same defect.
- **#60 — long action menus run off the pane** with no paging, scrolling or
  "…N more" marker. Found by the Competitor (C10) on a 35-option mulligan-bottom
  menu; the Rules Lawyer (L13) independently hit the more severe form, where a
  25-option Ghost Quarter target list pushed every castable spell in hand below
  the cut and locked the player out of casting for the rest of the game. That
  instance is a comment on the issue rather than a duplicate.
- **#61 — activated/loyalty ability menu entries don't name the ability**, so
  Olivia Voldaren's two abilities render as byte-identical lines and Liliana
  shows only "loyalty ability 0/1". The #36 fix disambiguated by *target*; this
  is the remaining half. Worst case seen: Grimgrin produced 13 consecutive
  identical entries. Found by the Competitor (C10).
- **#53 — the shortcut-hint row and the echoed input line are not clipped to
  width.** Already broken at the crew's default 120 columns (it eats the border
  and the card panel); below ~90 it wraps and destroys the `>` prompt. A long
  paste leaves permanent on-screen garbage. Found by the Vandal (V11).
- **#54 — the post-mulligan bottoming menu lists visually identical duplicate
  options** (10 of 35, when the hand holds two copies of a card), which is also
  what pushes that menu past the bottom of the pane. Found by the Vandal (V15).
- **#55 — unknown/misspelled CLI flags are silently ignored**, so a typo'd
  `--deck-1` quietly plays the built-in `red-green` deck, and `--help` / `-h` /
  `--version` launch a real interactive game instead of printing usage. Low
  severity, but it silently corrupts the repro provenance of any automated
  finder that shells out to this binary. Found by the coordinator in pre-flight.
- **#63 — mulligans are hard-capped at 3**, which CR 103.4 does not cap. Filed
  as a rules-parity note rather than a defect: both the Competitor (C10) and the
  Vandal (V15) hit it independently, both judged it a deliberate simplification,
  and the UI advertises the cap honestly as `N/3`. Everything within the cap is
  correct. Close as working-as-intended if that's the answer.

- **#66 — `none` at DECLARE ATTACKERS is auto-corrected rather than refused.**
  With a "attacks each combat if able" creature able to attack and nothing
  stopping it, the empty set is an illegal declaration (CR 508.1d); the engine
  accepts the input and forces the attacker in. It does write a
  `Forced attackers:` line to the LOG panel — this was first reported to the
  coordinator as happening silently, and the Rules Lawyer corrected itself after
  capturing a real transcript. The substantive complaint is the inconsistency:
  attack declarations get auto-corrected while block declarations one turn later
  in the same game are outright refused with the prompt redisplayed. May well be
  working as intended; filed so the two paths get made to match. Found by the
  Rules Lawyer (L12).

## Clean missions (no bugs found)

**C12** (mill race), **C13** (flyers vs ground stall), **C14** (topdeck war),
**L14** (timing and priority enforcement) and **L15** (attachment legality and
SBAs) completed their full game counts with no findings.

**L14 deserves calling out**: ~350 stepped prompts across two games, probing for
any land played off-turn or with a non-empty stack, any sorcery-speed spell
offered at instant speed, any loyalty ability outside its window or twice in a
turn, and any skipped or doubled priority window. It came back completely clean.

## Two missions did not exercise what they were designed to test

Recording these so the deck pairings get fixed rather than the missions being
marked done:

- **C12 never tested the decking rule.** `decks/coverage/ub-coverage.txt` has no
  Curse of the Bloody Tome and no Undead Alchemist (those are in the WU coverage
  deck); its only mill is one Nephalia Drownyard and one Selhoff Occultist,
  against a gw-humans deck that kills by turn 10–15. Neither game got a Drownyard
  into play, so CR 104.3c / 704.5b (lose on the *draw* from an empty library, not
  when the library empties) was never reached. The mission needs WU coverage as
  the mill seat, or a Drownyard-heavy list against a slower opponent.
- **L13 could not test the classic ordering trick** — killing Fiend Hunter in
  response to its own ETB trigger. Neither wu-coverage nor ub-zombies has
  instant-speed removal that can kill a 1/3 (Rebuke only hits attackers, Blazing
  Torch deals 2, ub-zombies has no instant removal at all). Wants a pairing with
  Doom Blade or similar.
- Similarly, **L15 could not make an attachment illegal via protection or
  hexproof** granted *after* attaching — wr-coverage's Angelic Overseer never
  coexisted with a Human, and Spectral Rider's protection-from-black had no black
  aura or equipment to test against.

The mission menu entries for C12, L13 and L15 now carry these deck requirements.

## Notable moments

- The Rules Lawyer verified **CR 400.7** properly: an Unruly Mob enchanted with
  Curiosity, equipped with Runechanter's Pike and buffed to 7/1 was exiled by
  Fiend Hunter, and came back as a **1/1, summoning sick, with no aura and no
  equipment** — Curiosity having gone to *its owner's* graveyard (CR 704.5m)
  while the Pike stayed on the battlefield unattached (CR 704.5n). Both halves
  of that distinction are right.
- The layer engine held up under everything stacked on it: Angelic Overseer at
  5/3 base + Glorious Anthem + Silver-Inlaid Dagger (correctly *not* the extra
  Human bonus — it is an Angel) + Wooden Stake came to exactly **9/4**. Removing
  an anthem by transforming Mayor of Avabruck into Howlpack Alpha recomputed
  every affected creature in the same instant rather than un-adding a stale
  number.
- Blocker eligibility was correct in **every combat across all 30 games**,
  including the subtle case of Stromkirk Noble ("can't be blocked by Humans")
  being unblockable by a Human Reckless Waif and then blockable once the Waif
  transformed into the non-Human Merciless Predator.
- All three crews chased suspected bugs that turned out to be correct rules and
  said so rather than filing them: Gavony Township really does cost `{2}{G}{W}`,
  Rally the Peasants really is `{2}{W}` with red flashback, Dream Twist really is
  an Instant, Skirsdag High Priest really taps *creatures* not Humans, Make a
  Wish really is random with no target choice, and a 1/1 Creepy Doll really does
  survive 4 marked damage (indestructible). The Vandal also disproved its own
  suspected "auto-pass eats your land drop" by replaying the raw terminal capture
  frame by frame — its own batched keystrokes had consumed those prompts.
- The Vandal's 1000-duplicate-block paste produced a single **52 KB** game-log
  line written into the save file, but combat damage itself was applied
  correctly — the 3/3 died to one 4/4 block, no damage multiplication.

## Observations recorded but not filed

- The engine auto-passes a priority window when a player's only remaining options
  are mana abilities, so such windows never appear on screen. No player choice is
  lost, so this is a UI shortcut rather than a rules break — but it is why steps
  can seem to vanish, and it cost two separate crews time before they pinned it
  down.
- `+3` selects menu option 3, because Rust's integer parser accepts a leading `+`.
- `Ctrl-C` exits 0, so a script cannot distinguish an interrupt from a finished
  game.
