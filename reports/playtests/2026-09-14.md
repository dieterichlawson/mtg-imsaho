# Playtest night — 2026-09-14

**Subject**: the harness (the LLM interface), chosen from the ledger rather than
from memory. Deriving the per-subject last nights out of
`reports/playtests/LEDGER.md` gave drafting (D) 09-13, the CLI (V/M) 09-12, the
game (C/L) 09-11 and the harness (H) 09-10 — so the harness had gone longest
without a night. Reading the ids actually run out of the ledger
(`H1 H2 H3 H4 H5 H6 H8 H9 H10`) against the ids in `docs/playtest/harness.md`
left **H7, H11, H12 and H13 untried**; three of them were taken.

**Crew**: 3 probes. **H7** run here; **H11** and **H13** run in parallel as
subagents, each on its own `logs/playtest/h11-*` / `h13-*` prefix.

**Runs**: roughly 510 games through the real `mtg-runner` plus one
`mtg-draft-runner` draft and tournament, and about 6,150 captured structured
requests. **No metered API seat was spawned at any point**: every game was
answered by a local `CLAUDE_CODE_BIN` stub, the draft run passed `--model cc`
explicitly (the flag defaults to `claude`), and the only real `claude`
invocations were three direct `--json-schema` probes on the CLI's own login.

**Issues**: 8 filed — #491–#498.

The night's shape: **the harness's schemas are sound and its games always end;
what is wrong is the prose around the schema and the cost of one decision.**
Two of the three probes went looking for a structural failure and found the
structure holding. H13 built the prompt nothing had ever reached and its schema
was clean, along with every other schema on both request paths. H11 muted nine
prompt kinds one at a time and every game still ended. In both cases the defect
was one layer out from where the idea pointed: in the *text* the seat reads, and
in the axis nobody was counting.

## The three that matter

**#491 — four decision prompts still bypass `build_prompt`, and the smallest
is 48 characters.** #463 routed the cleanup discard through `build_prompt`
because it was the only decision made with no board, and left a comment saying
so: *"this was the one prompt built from a format string alone."* It was not.
`prompt_target_selection`, both sacrifice prompts and the ability-target prompt
build their message with a bare `format!` and hand it straight to
`send_message_structured` — 59 of 417 requests, and the whole stdin for one of
them is:

```
Geistflame: select a target:
0: opponent, 1: you
```

A damage spell aimed at a player, with neither life total in the prompt. The
seat pointed it at itself eight times to five. The equip choice is the same
shape and the choice is the whole decision — five byte-identical Walking
Corpses, no word on which is tapped, damaged, already equipped or blocking. The
other surface settles it: the CLI asks the identical question through
`run_target_chooser`, which renders the entire paged board plus the log pane and
offers `s`, `g` and `i`, and its own comment says why — *"a player wants their
graveyard exactly when choosing a target (issue #122)."*

**#496 — one declare-blockers decision costs 20 model calls, and then throws
the answer away.** Both bounds on a game are counted in *decisions*: the
progress watchdog's `STALLED_DECISIONS = 100` and `mtg-runner`'s
`max_actions`. `choose_blockers_structured` holds a loop inside one decision —
`max_retries = 20`, each retry a full `send_message_structured`, each of those
up to `MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3` subprocesses bounded only by the 300s `CALL_TIMEOUT`.
That is 60 launches and about five hours on one prompt, and the watchdog
observes it once, on a game that is not stalled. Measured: **80 of one game's
98 calls were retries of 4 decisions, 85% of its prompt bytes and 72% of its
wall clock**. It re-sends the same prompt with the same schema, which still
offers the menace-illegal index, and after exhaustion declares no blocks —
discarding the legal blocks `mtg-engine/src/combat.rs:135-147` would have kept
from the identical answer.

**#494 — the action list names a planeswalker's ability by its index.**
`format_single_action` has no `ActivateLoyaltyAbility` arm, so the row falls
through to `other => format!("{other}")` and the engine's `Display`, which
drops the `targets`:

```
Activate loyalty ability 2 on obj#1
Activate loyalty ability 2 on obj#1
```

No name, no loyalty cost, no effect text, and "−6 at the opponent" and "−6 at
yourself" are byte-identical. It is the only `Display`-produced label of 367
shapes in a 2,609-request harvest, and 33 of the harvest's 35 duplicated-row
prompts. The seat took the self-targeting copy, divided its own 24 Swamps, and
sacrificed all of them. Same class as #460 and for the same reason: the CLI does
the `view.loyalty_abilities` lookup and appends a targets suffix (#61), and the
fix never crossed to this surface.

## H7 — the display-index vs legal-index audit

Every index in the program is faithful, and the audit's own headline is clean.
Over 11 stubbed games (1,038 structured requests, 9 top-level shapes, 0 illegal
schema keys) **145 of 145** activations named the copy its row promised,
including 32 that landed inside an `ActionRow::Copies` range and 32 at its far
end. #209's concede guard is intact with the display list collapsed: 68 of 68
priority menus asked `confirm_concede`, 68 cancels produced `PassPriority`, and
the game ended by decking on turn 68 with zero concessions. The other
index-vs-list suspects cleared by construction — `choose_card_set` displays
`view.your_hand` and indexes `prompt.options`, but both are
`state.objects_in_zone(Zone::Hand, player)` in that order; the attackers and
blockers prompts build their enums and apply their answers to the same slices;
`choose_x_funding`'s card-name keys are bucket keys, so unique, and nested one
level down; and the engine already excludes a spell from its own target list
(`targeting.rs:685`, `filter(|&id| id != spell_id)`).

What paid was a different diff: **`GAME_RULES` against the schemas actually
emitted**, rather than reading either alone. The system prompt tells every seat
the exile-from-graveyard prompt answers with *"a boolean per card; set true to
exile it, false to keep"* when the schema is an `indices` array (#492) — and
`mark_indices`' own doc comment says the opposite of the const, three functions
away. Five worked examples still show the comma-joined action list the program
stopped emitting (#493). Nothing checks that const against its own formatters;
`omitted_events_marker` is the one place that does.

## H11 — forward progress as a property

Nine prompt kinds muted one at a time (a successful call with no
`structured_output`) over ~490 games: **every arm exited cleanly**, none hit its
wall-clock budget. Eight of the ten callers substitute something legal and
cheap — no attackers, no blocks, keep, the first `min` cards, the listed order,
X=0, all in pile 2, index 0 — so the engine is never asked to refuse.
`choose_object_set` fills its own shortfall from the options, which matters
because `ChooseObjectSet` is the one refusal the engine answers by re-asking
rather than by cancelling a cast. And #462's residue — the `mark_indices`
shortfall — now trips the watchdog at 100 identical decisions and dies with a
report naming the seat and the question (ur/bg seed 11, exit 1, 4.7s, turn 24).

Worth knowing before designing an arm: `progress_fingerprint` is board-only, it
keeps just the previous value and resets on any change, so **any cycle of
period ≥ 2 is invisible to it by construction**. The prompt that would exploit
that is the mulligan, whose every refusal reshuffles and redraws — bounded today
by `mulligan_is_dominated` in one seat rather than by the runner. Two candidates
the idea named turned out vacuous: pile division was never reached at all
(confirming H13), and no card in the pool demands X ≥ 1. The third surface came
free: the fuzzer's seat ignores `legal_blocks` and `min_blockers` outright, so
11 of 255 declarations were blocks the engine silently dropped and a menace
minimum is never satisfied on purpose (#497). And `--check-invariants`' only
"the game is stuck" line can never execute — `engine.rs:1121/1276`
short-circuit `offers_nothing()` before every callback, and the branch they take
instead calls no callback, counts no action and logs nothing (#498).

## H13 — the prompt kinds nothing reaches

H13 was right that nothing reached `choose_pile_division` and right that the
release binary checks nothing: `Cargo.toml` has no `[profile.release]`, so both
`debug_assert!`s are compiled out and the harvest is the whole enforcement.
Built for the first time — `4 Liliana of the Veil / 56 Swamp` vs `60 Island`,
seed 13, and a stub policy that *wants* the ultimate: cast at 3 loyalty, `+1`
three times, `−6` around turn 19 — it was reached three times a game in a
release build, along with `confirm_concede`, which no label-reading policy will
ever reach because every seat is written to avoid `Concede`. That is all ten
callers plus both draft schemas.

The audit came back **clean on every check** across 2,609 requests and 12 caller
shapes over both request paths: 0 illegal top-level keys, 0 empty enums, 0
`maxItems: 0`, 0 `minItems > maxItems`, 0 `required` field missing from
`properties`, 0 of 26 `mark_indices` count-notes disagreeing with their
`minItems`/`maxItems`, 0 of 13 X-funding prompts disagreeing with their own
stated X range, and 0 index enums naming a row the prompt body does not print.
One direct `claude -p --json-schema` call with the real 24-key harvested pile
schema came back with a complete answer, so the nested card-name keys — spaces,
`#`, and now `/` from the `0/0` suffix — are accepted *and* answerable. The
opponent's side of a pile division is not a structured prompt at all:
`ChoosePile` is two enumerated `ChosenIndex` rows through `pick_action_index`.

16 of 17 `ResolutionChoiceKind` variants were raised; Nevermore's
`ChooseCardName` took its own `4 Nevermore / 56 Plains` deck and is 253 numbered
rows against a 253-value enum. The defects are both in the prose: #494 above,
and the pile prompt the seat then reached says only *"set true for pile 1, false
for pile 2"* — naming neither whose permanents these are nor that the target
player chooses a pile to sacrifice, which is the fact that inverts the answer,
while every land reads `0/0` (#495).

## Smaller observations, not filed

- The exile-cost instruction line is ungrammatical — `verb = "exile this card"`
  interpolated into `"Name the cards to {verb}."` gives *"Name the cards to
  exile this card."* Folded into #492 as the same fix site.
- Four code comments in `llm.rs` (`:3465`, `:3479`, `:3489`, `:3491`) still
  describe the boolean-per-card shape. Not seat-facing, so noted in #492 rather
  than filed.
- The attackers schema uses `{"type": "integer", "minimum": 0}` while
  `choose_x_funding` encodes integers as string enums with a comment saying
  Anthropic rejects `minimum` on integer fields. A `claude -p --json-schema`
  probe showed the CLI path accepts *and* answers it, so the `cc` seat is fine;
  the metered path is untestable here. Filed as no issue and scheduled as H17
  instead, because one of the two is wrong and it bites a seat this crew may
  not run.
- The draft sanitizer strips `thoughts` unconditionally while the game's `cc`
  backend keeps it (#213). Consistent with the draft's system prompt, and the
  draft records reasoning nowhere on any backend, so it is not the regression it
  looks like.

## What the night taught the guide

Four ideas added and one existing idea extended. **H14** (the budget for one
decision, from #496), **H15** (the refusals the watchdog can never see, from
H11's mulligan arm), **H16** (the seventeenth prompt — `ChooseDamageEffect`, the
one `ResolutionChoiceKind` that 6,150 requests never raised), **H17** (the same
schema, two providers, from the `minimum` asymmetry above), and a wrinkle on
**H12**: the sharper question is not which fields one label table names that the
other drops, but which `Action` variants have no arm in `format_single_action`
at all and land in the `Display` fallback — #494 was one, and the tell is that
the fallback drops a field the row then cannot express.

The method note worth keeping, and it is the same note from two directions: on
this subject the *schema* is now the well-tested half and the *prose* is not.
Both of H13's findings and two of H7's came out of diffing what the seat is told
against what the program actually emits — `GAME_RULES` against the live schemas,
and the action-list labels against the CLI's table — and neither diff needs a
game longer than a few seconds. A record of every `(prompt, schema)` pair plus a
`grep` for a duplicated row and for a `Display`-shaped label audits the whole
surface in one pass, which is worth more per minute than any amount of play.
