# 2026-09-28 — Drafting

Subject chosen from the ledger: **drafting** had gone longest without a
night (2026-09-23; the GUI had one on 09-27, the CLI on 09-26, the game
on 09-25, the harness on 09-24). Of `drafting.md`'s D1-D23, the ids the
ledger has never seen are D18, D19, D20, D21 and D23. Three probes:
**D20**, **D18** and **D19**.

Everything ran under one `CLAUDE_CODE_BIN` stub at an absolute path
(#540), `--model cc` on every invocation, no metered seat at any point.
The stub answers picks as a pure function of `sha256(system_prompt ‖
message)` so the same seed is the same draft, builds a 23-spell/17-land
deck from the schema's own enums, and answers the game phase by taking
the `Concede` row when the action menu offers one — D22's method note,
without which a multi-seat tournament is tens of thousands of calls.
Twenty-two runs in all, ~19,000 stub invocations.

## D20 — does the prompt change when the draft does

**Mostly negative, and the one gap is below the range #485 fixed.**

#485's defect is gone and gone properly. Running the same `--seed 7` at
every `--players` from 2 to 8 (and at 12, off the end of the collation's
range) and hashing what each seat was handed:

- one distinct system prompt per seat at every pod size, each carrying
  its own index and the real pod size (`You are seat 3 of an 8-seat
  pod, drafting against 7 other seat(s)`);
- the wheel prose is arithmetically right, not just parameterised. At
  `--players 8` the seat is told "a pack you pass comes back to you 8
  picks later, with the 7 cards the other seats took gone", and seat 0's
  pack 1 went 14 cards at pick 1 → 6 cards at pick 9, which is 7 gone
  since it was passed. The `pod_size == 2` branch's "exactly one card
  gone" checks out the same way (14 → 12 across two picks, one of them
  the seat's own);
- every `after your pick this pack passes <DIR> to seat N` claim was
  checked against the pack the named seat was actually handed on its next
  pick: **1,365 handoffs across pods 2-8 and all three pack rounds, 0
  real mismatches.** One compared unequal and is the checker's artifact —
  a pack with two Walking Corpses, where the index removed one copy and
  the runner the other, same multiset. A re-probe should compare sorted;
- `Pick N of 14` and `Available (15-N cards)` agreed on all 1,974 pick
  prompts, and every seat made 42 picks at every pod size including 12.

`--guide` routing is right in a fresh run: `--guide global --guide-1
alpha --guide-3 beta` at `--players 4` put GLOBAL/ALPHA/GLOBAL/BETA in
front of seats 0-3, 42 picks each, no bleed. (#580's padding defect is a
*resume* defect and this does not contradict it.)

#481 holds under the wrinkle the idea asked for. A `--save` snapshot
truncated in place to 24 picks — pack 1 pick 6, mid-pack-round — resumes
into a first prompt that already restates a 6-card pool, and the pool
size tracked `(pack-1)*14 + pick-1` for all 144 remaining picks. The
first call of each seat opens a fresh `--session-id` and every call after
it `--resume`s it, which is the prompt carrying the state rather than the
session, exactly as #481's fix intended.

What is left is the pod below the range #485 was written for. `--players
1` is accepted (`count()` refuses only 0), drafts a full 42-card pool and
exits 0, and the seat falls into `Table::description`'s `else` arm: it is
told it is "drafting against 0 other seat(s)", that a pack comes back "1
picks later, with the 0 cards the other seats took gone", that it should
read signals off cards nobody upstream took, and — 42 times — that "after
your pick this pack passes LEFT to seat 0". `total_rounds` does handle
one player (0 rounds, deliberately), but nothing says so: the log prints
an empty `TOURNAMENT` header straight into `FINAL STANDINGS` and a row
reading `1. Seat 0 — 0-0 (0 game wins)`, which is what a seat that played
and went even looks like. **#608.**

## D18 — the draft's own copy of the harness contract

**#213 never travelled, and it is the sharpest thing the night found.**

D18 asks for every place the two schema builders and prompt texts have
drifted. The one that costs something: `ClaudeCodeDraftBackend::decide`
runs every pick and deck schema through `sanitize_schema_for_anthropic`
(`llm_client.rs:399`), which has no `keep_thoughts` parameter and deletes
the `thoughts` property and its `required` entry unconditionally — while
the game side's `ClaudeCodeBackend::send_with_schema` calls
`sanitize_schema(schema, true)` with a comment naming #213 and saying
"This is the seat the project actually runs."

Captured off the stub's argv in one `--players 2 --best-of 1 --model cc
--seed 7` run:

```
draft pick  : {"additionalProperties":false,"properties":{"pick":{…,"enum":[0..13],
               "type":"integer"}},"required":["pick"],"type":"object"}
draft deck  : properties ["lands","maindeck"]   required ["maindeck","lands"]
game action : properties ["action","thoughts"]  required ["thoughts","action"]
```

86 of 86 draft calls got a schema with no `thoughts`; 202 of 202 game
calls got one with it. And `STRUCTURED_RESPONSE_FORMAT`, appended to
every draft backend's system prompt, tells the seat its reasoning "happens
in the model's extended-thinking channel" and that it must not emit a
`thoughts` key — a sentence true of the Anthropic and Gemini backends and
false in both halves for `claude -p`, which has no thinking block the
harness reads. That is #213's finding 3 verbatim.

The consequence, measured with a schema-obeying stub (emit only the keys
the schema declares, as a constrained decode would): **0 reasoning
records across all 86 draft calls, 202 across the tournament's game calls,
same run, same log, same binary, same seat.** A pick's whole durable
account is `{"pick": 13}` followed by `PICK Pack 1 Pick 1 | Chose:
Ulvenwald Mystics (from 14 cards)`. The `description` strings
`pick_schema_for` and `deck_schema_for` write — "Concise but complete
summary of your reasoning for this pick" — reach nobody. **#607.**

Two things checked and **correct** while in there, so nobody re-files
them: both copies guard the top-level-key rule with `debug_assert!`
(#398), which is a deliberate "loud for a developer, retry path for a
live game" choice stated in both comments and not an accident of one; and
the deck-build prompt is no longer #487's name/count list — it states the
40-card floor, the 17/23 target, the two-colour norm, that the leftovers
are a sideboard nothing is swapped from, and where a basic land goes. The
1-in-10-retry loop (`max_retries = 10`, each retry prefixing the
validator's own message) is intact.

## D19 — the drawn game

**#484's fix holds, and the drawn *match* is reachable after all — not
through a drawn game, but through an even `--best-of`.**

The idea's premise is out of date: `play_match`'s loop is now
`while !match_is_over(best_of, games.len(), wins_a, wins_b)` with the
`games_played >= best_of` clause MTR 6.5 wants, and `MatchFormat`'s
`n.min(3)` is gone.

The drawn *game* is still unreached and looks unreachable from this pool.
The engine's only draw is `sba.rs:369`'s "zero players alive"; no card in
`data/oracle_cache.json` deals damage to each player or makes each player
draw, so there is no simultaneous loss to build; 12 `random` vs `random`
games at seeds 1-31 ended 9 on life total and 3 on an empty library, none
simultaneous. `engine.rs:1420`'s "the game is stuck" draw is documented as
believed unreachable.

But a drawn *match* does not need one. At an even `--best-of`,
`wins_needed(2) = 2` and `wins_needed(4) = 3`, so a level score is a draw,
and the whole chain the idea asks about fires:

```
MATCH Round 1 — Seat 0 vs Seat 1: 2-2 (Draw)
1. Seat 0 — 0-0-2 (4 game wins)
```

`--best-of 2` stopped every match at 2 games and `--best-of 4` at 4 (8
and 16 games for 4 matches), `MatchResult::winner()` returned `None`,
`Standing::match_draws` was incremented for both seats, and
`match_points` gave 2. `--best-of 1/3/5` are right too (1, 3 and 5 games,
`2-3`/`3-2` at five) and the `## Play/draw` section says "best-of-4 …
Games 2 to 4" at each, so #210 and #484's prompt half are both closed.

What the probe did find is what the seat is *not* told. `play_match`
re-inits both conversations per game and passes only
`MatchFormat::BestOf(best_of)`, a constant. Hashing the `--system-prompt`
of every game-phase call in the `--players 4 --best-of 4` run: **3,232
calls, 4 distinct prompts, 808 each — one per seat**, differing only in
the decklist section. Game 1 and game 4 of a match, round 1 and round 2, a
0-0 match and a 2-1 match: identical bytes. So a seat cannot know it is in
game 2 — which is the one rule `## Play/draw` spends five lines teaching —
and cannot know that at `--best-of 2` a level finish is a draw worth 1
point against 3. Four matches drew in that run and no seat knew a draw was
available. **#609.**

## Method notes for the next drafting night

- **`--best-of 2` or `--best-of 4` is how you reach a drawn match**, and
  it costs nothing. D8 and D19 both went looking for a drawn *game*; the
  match-level path needs only an even format.
- **A conceding stub keeps the tournament phase affordable** (D22's note,
  confirmed): `--players 4 --best-of 4` is 3,232 game calls and about 40
  seconds with it.
- **Compare pack residues as sorted multisets, not tuples.** ISD packs
  can hold two copies of a common, and the runner does not remove the copy
  your index names.
- A `--save` snapshot's `picks` array can be truncated in place with
  `json.load`/`json.dump` and resumed; `round` and `pick` are 1-based and
  filtering on them lands you exactly mid-pack-round.
