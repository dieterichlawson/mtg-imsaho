## The night's shape

**The page is a sound machine and an unreliable narrator.** Across ~20 games
it never panicked, never hung, never offered a decision its widgets could not
answer, and never broke a rule: 0 page errors, 0 invariant violations, 0
panics. Of 22 issues, **20 are the page's** and **two are the engine's**, and
neither engine issue has a GUI symptom at all — one had to be provoked with an
action no shipped seat sends, and the other was found by `random`-vs-`random`
with no browser involved.

What breaks, over and over, is what the page *tells you*: what it draws past
the edge of itself, what it declines to say when it refuses you, what
vocabulary it uses for the two players, and what it decides on your behalf
without a word.

Three clusters:

**1. Nothing bounds a row.** The battlefield row's only concession to a
crowded board is a stride that shrinks to a 10px floor and then simply keeps
going (#513). Card 45 crosses the pane edge; on a real seed-11 game with
Army of the Damned, 108 Zombies drew to x=1117 in a 640px frame with **60 of
them painted and then covered by the side panel** — invisible, uncounted, and
still answering clicks. The same `rowLayout` does it to the hand at 42
(comment on #513). The page *has* a collapse mechanism and applies it to
basic lands only, so identical tokens — the one thing that reaches these
counts — are exactly what it never collapses, while the CLI has collapsed
them since #508. The band's turn/step line is the one string on the board
drawn without `clip()` (#522), and the log drawer prints its heading and its
first line on the same baseline (#521).

**2. Silence where the other three surfaces speak.** The mark widget's Enter
refuses below the minimum without a word and commits an empty set at `min 0`
(#518, #520, #524) — #262's rule, which the CLI's set screen was built
around. A library search shows 11 of 30 cards with no pager, no scroll and no
count (#529). The page never says which seat you are, so its own game-over
headline and every `p0`/`p1` log line are undefined vocabulary (#519) — the
CLI prints `you are p0` for this exact reason (#115) and the LLM seat rewrites
the tokens away (#465). The band shows the last two log lines rather than what
changed since you last acted, and 41% of prompts arrive with more than two new
lines (#523).

**3. The inspector and the list, where the Reader lives.** The page's
inspector drops three facts the CLI's `i` page carries — colour (#357), the
regeneration-shield count (#468), and what is attached to the creature (#83)
— and prints `(printed 0/0)` for a `*/*` creature because `PermanentView`'s
`star_pt` reaches the browser and is never read (#526, which is #267 on the
fourth surface). An activated ability on the stack overwrites its own source
permanent in the page's index, so hovering the Ghoulcaller's Bell on the
battlefield shows you the ability instead (#527), and never shows the source's
art because `artFor` strips at the first `'s ` and an ability is named
`X ability` (#528). Worst of the group: a library search draws 11 of its 30
rows with no pager, no scroll and no count, so **19 legal answers the engine
offered cannot be sent at all** (#529) — and the row budget is elastic in the
wrong direction, since hovering a card with long oracle text pushes the prompt
down and deletes rows from the bottom. The filter box compounds it: typing
after scrolling empties the modal, because the wheel clamps against the
*unfiltered* row count (#530), and the box you type into is not the box the
page draws — the real input floats at the top of the canvas over the
opponent's life strip while the modal paints an inert one (#531).

**4. Two tabs are two players.** Every tab auto-answers plain priorities on
its own, so `s` in the tab you are using is defeated by any other tab (#515);
and because the protocol has no "that decision was answered" message, the tab
that did not answer keeps the dead prompt live and throws your click away in
silence (#516). #71's actual rule — a stale answer never lands on a newer
prompt — **held** under every attack: stale seqs, two tabs racing the same
live seq, close-and-reopen mid-prompt. It is the bookkeeping around the drop
that is missing, not the drop.

## #514 and #533 — the two engine issues, from opposite directions

`mtg-engine/src/engine.rs:1399` computes `legal` for the prompt and then calls
`submit_action` **without ever comparing the answer against it**. Answer a
declare-attackers prompt with `PassPriority` and the pass is not ignored — it
is executed. Priority moves to the non-active player, the prompt is re-asked,
and `invariants/prompts.rs:51` (CR 508.1) fires: `attackers prompt but
priority is Some(PlayerId(1)), not the active player's`, exit 2. Deterministic
on 3/3 seeds, all at turn 2, with `p0 passes priority` as the game log's last
line.

The same hole at a `ResolutionChoice` prompt is worse: passing while Fiend
Hunter's ETB target choice is outstanding lets the step machinery advance past
it, and the queued triggers survive into combat — `attackers prompt with
triggers still queued`, turn 7, exit 2.

This is invisible to the fuzzer by construction: the random seat only ever
picks from `legal`, so `--p1 random --p2 random` on the same pairing is clean
on all five seeds tried. It took a seat that could say something the prompt
never offered, which is what a socket protocol with a `window.mtgSend` on the
other end is. No shipped seat reaches it — the page's widgets only send
`DeclareAttackers`, the CLI parses into the prompt, the LLM seat substitutes a
legal answer (#496) — so the severity is low and the shape of it is not.

**#533 is the mirror image**: found without a browser at all. Two Skeletal
Grimaces on one creature put the *identical* `ActivateAbility` into
`legal.actions` twice, tripping the `distinct_offers` invariant on 3 of 5
seeds of a plain `random`-vs-`random` mirror. The page happens to be immune —
`beginMenu` rebuilds its rows from `legal.activatable_abilities` rather than
from `legal.actions` — which is exactly why a GUI night found it and the CLI
and LLM seats, which do read `legal.actions`, have been showing the duplicate
all along. A good argument for the README's "one decision, four surfaces"
rule: the surface that is immune is the one that shows you the bug.

Two smaller findings round out the night: the inspector prints past the canvas
edge for 34 of 279 card names and a hand card silently drops its third wrapped
line (#532), and neither detail view — the page's inspector nor the CLI's `i`
— ever says that a permanent is a token (#534), which is the rare case where
the page and the CLI are wrong together.

## What held, and is worth not re-testing for a while

- The **zone overlay**: 137 cards → 3 pages of 50/50/37, nothing off-pane on
  any page, all 137 reachable, last card reachable, page index rebuilt fresh
  per open. #364's CLI failure mode has no counterpart here.
- **#71's stale-answer rule**, end to end, under three separate attacks.
- **`f`** (G8): 5/5 engagements across seeds 11, 5, 23 skipped the rest of the
  main phase and stopped at the next prompt with an accurate notice. (Only
  the half engaged at your *own* main phase — see the guide note added.)
- **G6**: nothing that is not highlighted answers a prompt by accident — the
  opponent's life band, a non-option land, a hand card, a zone counter,
  double-click, right-click and wheel during a pick all answered nothing.
- **Enter is the only key that passes a menu.**
- The **GAME OVER** panel dims the board rather than hiding it, so the final
  position stays readable behind it.
- The **combat-state facts #243/#297/#504 added**: a Vampire Interloper reads
  `ATTACKING YOU / CAN'T BLOCK`, a Curse reads `ENCHANTS OPPONENT`, a token's
  art and keywords are present, and a *trigger* on the stack does find its
  source's art (only an *activated* ability does not — #528).
- The **list modal** pages and counts correctly (`1–20 of 30 (scroll)`). It is
  the side panel's row list, a different path, that has no pager (#529).
- The page as a machine: ~20 games, 0 page errors, 0 panics, 0 invariant
  violations, and no decision the widgets could not answer.

And a note on method, since this was the subject's first night: the findings
that came fastest were not from clicking. They were from reading one file
against the contract it was meant to satisfy — `rowLayout` against "everything
printed fits", `inspector()` against `paint_permanent_detail`, `ask()` against
#71 — and then driving the disagreement. Three of the four probes found their
headline that way before they had played a full game.
