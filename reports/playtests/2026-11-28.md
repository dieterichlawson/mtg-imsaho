## V16: deck-file abuse

I read `load_deck_file` first. It splits on the first space, parses the count as a `u32`, trims with `str::trim`, and refuses fewer than 7 cards (#625). It has no upper bound. I ran 17 files:

- **Absurd counts parse and are built.** 1e5 Plains takes 0.6 s and 1e6 takes 6.3 s. 3e6 takes 20.7 s at a `VmHWM` of 7,069,332 kB, about 2.4 KB a card. 1e8 and 4294967295 are killed by the OOM killer with exit 137 and an empty stderr, and the container reported another process killed for memory at the same moment (**#1101**).
- **A UTF-8 BOM** survives `trim`. `40 Plains` is refused as `invalid count '40'`, with the BOM inside the quotes and invisible, and a BOM before a comment gives `invalid count '#'` (**#1102**).
- **Checked, correct:**
  - These are refused with the line number and exit 1: a tab separator (`invalid count '4<TAB>Lightning'`), a lowercase name, a `Sideboard` header, a `//` comment, a trailing `# basics`, all-zero counts, and Insectile Aberration (a back face, unknown).
  - These play: `+40`, `040`, two spaces, exactly 7 cards, and a DFC front face.
  - The draft's written decks (`write_deck_file`) are plain `COUNT NAME` lines with no sideboard section, so the runner reads every deck the hosted table writes.

## V23: combat-declaration syntax

I placed a board from seed 231: four p0 attackers and three p1 blockers, including Daybreak Ranger against Elite Inquisitor's protection from Werewolves. Then I fed both parsers in hotseat.

- **Attack prompt.** Refused: `0 0` and `+1 01` (duplicate), `all none`, `0:0`, `:0`, `0>`, `-1`, `1 x`, fullwidth `４`, `ALL`, `0;1` and `0.1`. `0>pw0` with no walker is explained, and a 23-digit index is reported out of range. Accepted: `0,,1` declares 0 and 1, and ` all ` is all.
- **Block prompt.** `1:1` says Daybreak Ranger can't legally block the Inquisitor, which is correct under CR 702.16. `0:0 0:1` cites 509.1b. `0:0:0`, `0:`, `:`, `0-0`, `all`, `a`, `x:y`, `0:0 junk` and `0：0` (fullwidth colon) are refused whole. `0:5` names the attacker range.
- **A rig artefact, not a bug.** A 4000- or 5000-character line sent with `tmux send-keys -l` lost its Enter. The line stayed in the echo, nothing was refused, and the next input was read alone. Up to 2000 characters were refused normally. The same 5000 digits sent as a bracketed paste (`tmux paste-buffer -p`) were refused at once. The keystroke flood overruns the pty's 4096-byte input queue, so this is the rig, not the reader. Added to V23 in `cli.md`.
