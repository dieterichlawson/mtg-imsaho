# What the draft guide changes: one night's picks, asked again

The draft loop's ledger compares guided and unguided seats by match
record, and after eighteen nights the two are level (guided 92-100 in
matches, unguided 124-116). A match record is noisy: twelve matches a side
a night, so an edge of a few points takes months to see. This asks a
narrower question with far less noise: **does the guide change what a
drafter picks, and how?**

## How

The 312 picks of the 2026-11-07 night (`nights/2026-11-07-positions.jsonl.xz`:
every pack a seat was asked to pick from, with the pool it had) were asked
again of the same drafter (`cc:claude-haiku-5-5`) with
`scripts/decision-eval/draft-replay.py`, four times over:

| run | guide |
| --- | --- |
| `none-a`, `none-b` | none (`--guide none`) |
| `first` | the first guide, 704 words (`guide.md` at dc04ee9d, after 2026-10-21) |
| `current` | the current guide, 1,966 words (`guide.md` at 73a2058e, after 2026-11-07) |

Two no-guide runs measure how often the drafter disagrees with itself.
A pick is *settled* where both agreed (261 of 312); a guide's effect is
counted there, so the drafter's own randomness is not credited to the
guide. `scripts/decision-eval/guide-effect.py` does the counting; the
answers are in `2026-11-07-results.tar.xz`. About 1,250 `claude -p`
calls, plan quota.

## What it found

**The guide changes a lot of picks.** Without a guide the drafter
disagrees with itself on 16% of picks. Either guide changes a third of
the settled picks:

| | all picks differ from `none-a` | settled picks changed |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `none-b` (noise) | 16% | — |
| `first` | 39% | 33% |
| `current` | 38% | 34% |

By stage of the pack (settled picks): picks 1-4 change 34-35%, picks 5-9
40-43%, picks 10-14 19-26%. Guided and unguided seats' packs change
alike (32% and 35%), so it is the guide, not the seat's own history.

**The drafter follows what the guide says.** The two guides disagree with
each other on 40% of picks, and each pushes its own cards:

- The first guide (green-first, written from two nights) takes Orchard
  Spirit +10, Villagers of Estwald +7, Boneyard Wurm +7, Night Revelers +5,
  Kindercatch +4 and Festerhide Boar +3 more often than no guide does.
- The current guide takes Tormented Pariah +6, Urgent Exorcism +5,
  Woodland Sleuth +4, Ghoulcaller's Chant +4, Curse of the Pierced
  Heart +4, Spider Spawning +3 and Skirsdag Cultist +3 more often, and
  Lost in the Mist, Cobbled Wings, Brain Weevil and Vampire Interloper
  less (−3 each).

Against the current guide's own lists, over all 312 picks:

| run | cheap removal taken | skip-list cards taken | mean deck win rate of cards taken |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `none-a` | 24 | 55 | 49.7% |
| `none-b` | 18 | 54 | 49.8% |
| `first` | 18 | 49 | 50.4% |
| `current` | 29 | 23 | 50.7% |

The current guide takes about a fifth more cheap removal and half as
many skip-list cards. Where it changed a settled pick and both cards have
30+ games in the ledger (48 picks), its card has the higher deck win rate
31 times and the lower 17.

## What it means

1. **The guide is not being ignored.** A third of picks move, toward the
   guide's own priorities. The level match record is therefore not because
   the drafter skips the guide.
2. **Whether the moved picks are better is not settled here.** The
   yardsticks above are the guide's own lists and the ledger's card
   numbers, which the guide is written from, so agreeing with them is
   partly circular. The mean deck win rate of the cards taken barely
   moves (49.7% to 50.7%), and a 1-point lift in the cards picked is
   well inside what three-game matches can show in eighteen nights.
3. **Picks may not be where games are won.** Deck building and play are
   unchanged by the guide. If those are the bottleneck, better picks would
   show here and not in the record.

## Next

- **Judge the changed picks.** The 88 settled picks the current guide
  changed (`changed.json` in the results) could each be shown with the
  pack and pool to a stronger model, asking which pick is better. That
  gives a yardstick the guide was not written from.
- **Compare candidate guides on these same picks**: a trimmed guide
  without the nightly anecdotes, and a short guide distilled from the
  conventional Innistrad wisdom, against the current one.
- **Replay a second night** to see whether these numbers hold.
