# Four draft guides on two nights' picks, judged

Follows `2026-11-07.md`, which found that the draft loop's guide changes
about a third of a night's picks toward its own priorities, but could not
say whether the changed picks were better. This adds three things:

1. **A judge** that does not use the guide's own numbers: Sonnet
   (`scripts/decision-eval/judge-picks.py`) is shown each changed pick's
   pack, the drafter's pool and the two cards, as A and B in a fixed
   pseudo-random order, and says which is the better pick. It never sees
   the guide or which card a guide chose.
2. **Two more guides**, asked over the same picks:
   - *trimmed*: the current guide with the one-night stories and the
     anecdotes cut (1,469 words against 1,966);
   - *conventional*: a short guide of standard Innistrad limited wisdom,
     written without the loop's statistics (about 700 words; it is in
     `2026-11-07-guides-results.tar.xz`).
3. **A second night**: 2026-11-06's 312 picks, asked with no guide (twice)
   and under the current, trimmed and conventional guides.

About 2,200 Haiku replays and 530 Sonnet judgements, all `claude -p` on
plan quota.

## How many picks each guide changes

Settled picks are those the drafter makes the same way in both no-guide
runs (261 on 11-07, 269 on 11-06); with no guide it disagrees with itself
on 16% and 14% of picks.

| guide | words | 11-07 settled picks changed | 11-06 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| first | 704 | 33% | — |
| current | 1,966 | 34% | 29% |
| trimmed | 1,469 | 35% | 26% |
| conventional | ~700 | 24% | 20% |

## Whether the changed picks are better

Two yardsticks, and they disagree.

**The judge** (both nights pooled; "better" is the share of decided
picks where the guide's card was judged the better pick, with a 95%
interval):

| guide | judged | guide's card better | worse | even | better |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| first | 86 | 28 | 53 | 5 | 35% ± 10% |
| current | 165 | 80 | 77 | 8 | 51% ± 8% |
| trimmed | 162 | 72 | 81 | 9 | 47% ± 8% |
| conventional | 117 | 65 | 44 | 8 | 60% ± 9% |

By night: current 55% (11-07) and 46% (11-06); trimmed 49% and 44%;
conventional 57% and 63%.

**The ledger** (where the guide changed a settled pick and both cards
have 30+ games, how often the guide's card has the higher deck win rate):

| guide | 11-07 | 11-06 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| first | 32 of 48 | — |
| current | 31 of 48 | 25 of 35 |
| trimmed | 37 of 50 | 25 of 35 |
| conventional | 18 of 36 | 7 of 22 |

Each guide does best by the yardstick it was built from. The loop's
guides are written from the ledger and agree with it; the conventional
guide is the standard view of the format, and Sonnet's judgement of
Innistrad picks is that same standard view, from the same writing.
Neither yardstick is this environment's truth: the ledger's card numbers
are a few points apart on 100-200 games and partly reflect which decks
play a card, and the judge works from what it remembers of the set (its
reasons sometimes get a card's cost or size wrong) and from how people
played the format, not how these AI seats play it.

## What it means

1. **The first guide made picks worse.** By the judge it is clearly below
   even (35% ± 10%), and its picks lean on cards like Orchard Spirit,
   Boneyard Wurm and Festerhide Boar from two nights of numbers. The loop
   has improved on it: the current guide's changes are even by the judge.
2. **The current guide is not shown to help or hurt picks.** Its changes
   are a coin flip by the judge (51% ± 8%) and favourable by the ledger.
   That fits the match record, which is level.
3. **Cutting the anecdotes did not help.** The trimmed guide changes a
   similar share of picks and is judged no better (47% ± 8%); the
   anecdotes are not what makes the guide's picks good or bad.
4. **The conventional guide changes fewer picks and the judge likes more
   of them (60% ± 9%)**, but the ledger dislikes them. Whether standard
   Innistrad wisdom beats the loop's guide *here* is the question the
   judge cannot answer, because it holds the same view. Only games can.
5. **The drafter misreads card names.** Under the conventional guide it
   took Traveler's Amulet five more times on 11-06, a card the guide never
   names; the guide praises Travel Preparations.

## Next

- **Settle it in games.** Run the conventional guide as a third arm of the
  nightly loop (say seats 0 and 4 with the loop's guide, 2 and 6 with the
  conventional one, odd seats with none). The ledger then says which guide
  wins matches in this environment, which is the question both yardsticks
  here are standing in for.
- **Tell the curator what to keep.** The loop's guide is even with no
  guide by the judge and favoured by the ledger; a curator told to write
  only where this environment differs from the standard view would give a
  guide whose changes the ledger supports and the judge has no prior on.
